## 0. One-paragraph summary of the organizing result

I mapped **14 classes of divine conception** (about as many as the world's major traditions define), classified every kind of claim inside them into six claim-types, audited **37 specific testable claims** (A1–A15, B1–B18, four cosmological rows) spanning manuscript philology, archaeology, datable prophecy, miracle and medical-psychology literature, and end-time predictions. The results are asymmetric in a way that matters: **the claims that can be tested almost never turn out to be the core metaphysical commitments**, and the core commitments are almost always formulated so that no test can touch them. Confirmed testable claims (e.g., the Pilate inscription, the "house of David" stele, Tyre's re-occupation) confirm a mundane historical/textual substrate, not a metaphysics; failed testable claims (e.g., William Miller's October 22, 1844 prediction) do not falsify an entire tradition's metaphysics either. The core question — *which conception of God, if any, is true?* — is structured so that (a) assertoric versions collapse into unfalsifiability, and (b) non-assertoric versions are not the kind of thing that can be proven. That is a claim about the evidential architecture of the problem, **not** a claim about the truth of any theism or atheism.

## 1. Logical skeleton: the structural features that make this question resist proof

These are the load-bearing points; the taxonomy (§2) and evidence ledger (§3) hang off them.

### L1. The assertion fork
A theological statement can only be adjudicated if it is **assertoric** (truth-valued). Two live traditions deny that:
- **Non-cognitivism** (theism-as-poetry): R. B. Braithwaite, *An Empiricist's View of the Nature of Religious Belief* (1955) — religious sentences express stories plus moral policies, not facts. If that is right, "proof" is a category error; nothing is being asserted to prove.
- **Attitude/use theories** (Wittgensteinian language-games, D. Z. Phillips): religious claims function in a form of life, not in an evidence game.
- **Apophatic theology** (Pseudo-Dionysius, *Mystical Theology*; Maimonides, *Guide of the Perplexed* I.52–59; Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I q.13) asserts that God is beyond predication — "God is not this, not that." Apophatic claims are **true-if-anything, false-if-anything**, i.e., empty of discriminating content.
- **A. J. Ayer** (*Language, Truth and Logic*, 1936) ran the fork the other way: since theism asserted nothing testable, it was meaningless. (The verification principle was widely judged self-undermining and revised; Strawson 1950. Both legs fail, but the fork itself is durable.)
- **Crucial half**: an assertoric version of a divinity claim *could* be adjudicated in principle — but only if it generates a discriminating commitment. See L3.

### L2. Falsifiability and immunization
- **Popper's demarcation criterion** (*Logik der Forschung*, 1934; Eng. *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*, 1959): a claim that no conceivable event could refute is not scientific, and (for practical evidence purposes) carries no confirmable content. [Classical metaphysics is not thereby *false*; it is thereby *untestable*.]
- **Flew's parable of the invisible gardener** ("Theology and Falsification," 1950 symposium with Hare and Mitchell; reprinted Flew & MacIntyre, eds., *New Essays in Philosophical Theology*, 1955): a believer whose God-intervention claim is repeatedly disconfirmed re-defines God ("he is invisible, scentless, no effect on the garden") until the claim is compatible with everything — and therefore with nothing.
- **Sagan's dragon in the garage** (*The Demon-Haunted World*, 1996): "a fire-breathing dragon that is invisible, incorporeal, floats, produces no heat" — the formal template of an unfalsifiable hypothesis.
- **Immunization is documented in the wild**: Hiram Edson's reinterpretation of the Great Disappointment (Oct 22, 1844 — the predicted Second Coming did not occur; within a day the doctrine became "investigative judgment *in heaven*," unobservable); Zygmunt, "Prophecy Failure and the Cultic Phenomenon," *ASR* 1972; Festinger, Riecken & Schachter, *When Prophecy Fails* (1956). When a testable datum fails, theology historically responded by making the claim less testable.
- **Lakatos** ("Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes," 1970): research programmes survive by theoretical adjustment; the diagnostic sign of degeneration is content-*loss* after prediction-failure, which is exactly the pattern above.

### L3. Underdetermination and the parity argument
For any proposed tradition T with divine being G:
1. Construct T′: identical in every observable consequence, different in second-order commitment (different name, conflicting revelations, different soteriology).
2. Since T and T′ predict the same evidence, no evidence e can discriminate them.
3. Every live tradition can be T′ for some other tradition T.
This "parity" point is the outsider-test intuition (Loftus, *The Outsider Test for Faith*, 2010) and is structurally similar to Duhem–Quine underdetermination. It is why convergent historical evidence for one tradition's mundane substrate (e.g., that a church exists, a manuscript is old, a named king existed) does not transfer to its metaphysics: the same substrate exists under rival metaphysics with rival T′s.

### L4. The Euthyphro dilemma (Plato, *Euthyphro* 10a)
"Is the pious loved by the gods because it is pious, or pious because loved by the gods?" In monotheistic form: (i) if X is good *because* God commands it, morality is arbitrary (God could command cruelty); (ii) if God commands X *because* X is good, then goodness is a standard external to and independent of God — so God is not the ground of value. Modern repair attempts: divine-command modified by God's *nature* (Robert M. Adams, *Finite and Infinite Goods*, 1999: God's nature is the Good, chosen arbitrariness replaced by essential-goods); Thomistic identity (God's essence *is* goodness — *ST* I q.6). The dilemma does not decide theology; it explains why moral arguments for a specific divine being cannot be proofs: **the goodness of "good" and the existence of "God" are then partly independent premises**, and any morality needs an independent anchor — which is the contested move.

### L5. Divine hiddenness
J. L. Schellenberg's formal argument (*Divine Hiddenness and Human Salvation*, 1993; *Wisdom to Doubt*, 2007): (1) if a perfectly loving God exists, there would be no non-resistant non-believers; (2) there are non-resistant non-believers; (3) so (there is no perfectly loving God). Responses:
- **CORNEA**: Wykstra, "The Humean Obstacle…," IJPR 1984 — we cannot presume to know the conditions under which a transcendent God would give evidence; skeptical theism; Bergmann 2001.
- **Distance pedagogy**: Hick (*An Interpretation of Religion*, 1989, 2nd ed. 2004) — cognitive distance is *required* for free response; hiddenness is a feature, not a bug.
- **Volitional transformation**: Moser — relationship follows character change, not evidence.
The decisive observation for this audit: **the hiddenness-saving move ("God would remain hidden") is itself unfalsifiable** — it converts any lack of evidence into evidence-compatible-with-theism, i.e., it immunizes (L2) rather than resolves.

### L5b. The problem of evil — logical vs evidential, and why it is prior-locked too

Two arguments are routinely conflated and must be separated:

1. **The logical problem** (Mackie, "Evil and Omnipotence," *Mind* 1955): omnipotence + omnibenevolence + extant evil is an inconsistent triad. The free-will defense (Plantinga, *God, Freedom, and Evil*, 1974) blocks the inconsistency claim by denying that we can know which greater-goods worlds an omnipotent, omnibenevolent being would be obliged to actualize; skeptical theism (Bergmann 2001) generalizes the point. Widely judged not to yield a formal contradiction.
2. **The evidential problem** (Rowe 1979/1984; Draper, "Pain and Pleasure," *Noûs* 1989): the observed distribution of suffering — intensity, cruelty, absence of observable merit-tracking, pre-human animal suffering — is claimed to be far more likely on naturalism than on theism. This is the live form, and it is *about likelihood ratios*, so in principle it is measurable.

**Why no observation adjudicates it.** Both sides stipulate the observable: suffering exists with its observed distribution, and P(pattern | naturalism) ≈ 1 by biology. The entire force of the argument sits in x = P(pattern | theism) — and the data cannot bound x, because "gratuitous" suffering is *defined* by the absence of a hidden justifying good, whose presence or absence is unobservable by construction. Wykstra's CORNEA noseeum inference (IJPR 1984) is exactly the argument that we have no handle on the conditions under which a transcendent God would render justifications visible. So x is a stipulated parameter, not a measurement, and the argument's strength is exactly the x a defender is willing to grant — a prior, not a datum.

**What would count as evidence, stated honestly:** an observable index of *gratuitousness* — a distribution of suffering demonstrably untethered from any (even hidden, probabilistically constrained) moral account, or a case such that every hidden-goods auxiliary of the CORNEA class is itself bounded below some price the data can check. None is known, and CORNEA predicts that none could be *recognized* even if present. That is the sense in which this argument-resister is like the others.

**Computed reach** (`god-religions-truth_evil_evidential_reach_calc.cjs`): across the full stipulation grid x ∈ {1, 0.99, 0.9, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05, 0.01}, the maximum |log₁₀ BF| is **2.0** (at the most hostile stipulation, x = 0.01): BF = 10⁻², erased by **one 100:1-class auxiliary** (the cheapest auxiliary class in the prayer-audit §7 / B14-rule §6 tax) and **5.70 orders of magnitude short** of the ledger's decisive-datum standard (three day-exact predictions, BF ≈ 10⁷·⁷). The argument only matches a null's *achieved* evidential weight (log₁₀ BF₀₁ ≈ 0.15) if the defender grants x ≥ 0.708; only the hostile cells x ≤ 0.1 clear that band, and each of those is erased by a single auxiliary.

**What it bears on.** Even total evidential success would bear on the *attribute conjunction* (omnipotence ∧ benevolence — claim-class B, §4), not on the existence, number, unity, or identity of the divine, which stay prior-locked (proof-bounds §1 R1, §5 R4). Process theology (§2) absorbs the data by conceding non-omnipotence and predicts nothing differentially. And the tax is symmetric: a naturalistic core declining hidden-goods reasoning pays the same κ (B14-rule §6). This section maps the argument's structure and its evidential reach; it does not adjudicate the metaphysics.

## 2. Taxonomy: 14 classes of divine conception (brief item 1)

Legend — **[TH]** historically testable · **[TP]** physically testable · **[SP]** sociologically/psychologically testable · **[NT]** non-testable (no possible evidence discriminates) · confidence: **H**igh/**M**edium/**L**ow.

### 2.1 Classical theism (the shared Abrahamic metaphysical core)
- **Number:** exactly one God, necessarily existing.
- **Nature/attributes:** creator ex nihilo; omnipotent, omniscient, omnibenevolent; eternal and timeless (Augustine; Aquinas *ST* I q.10); simple (I q.3); personal; immutable; source of moral goodness. [NT — all attributes, as formulated]
- **Testable hooks under this label across traditions:** [TH] existence/death of named figures; [TP] claimed miracles; [TH] prophecy and manuscript dating (§3).
- **Non-testable core:** "ground of being," "pure act," "beyond being," divine simplicity, the Trinity as metaphysics (is the three-in-one a *metaphysical* natures claim or an institutional definition? Cf. Nicaea 325; Chalcedon 451 — the church documents are testable as documents; the metaphysical content is not).

### 2.2 Judaism
- **Number:** one (Shema, Deut 6:4) — but textual history shows development: deity names, Asherah associations.
- **Nature:** YHWH, covenant-maker, non-incarnate; Torah given at Sinai; Kabbalistic **Ein Sof** = God beyond all description (apophatic) [NT].
- **Testable hooks:** [TH] Sinai-Pentateuch composition-hypothesis (the Documentary Hypothesis — JEDP; Wellhausen, 19th c.; modern modifications — e.g., Van Seters 1992/2015; Rendtorff) — a *philological* testable program; [TH] Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions (8th c. BCE) invoking "YHWH of Samaria and his Asherah" (see §3.1) — historical evidence that early practice was monolatristically open or polytheistic [TH, debated M]; [NT] covenant with the one true creator.

### 2.3 Christianity
- **Number:** one God in three persons (Trinity).
- **Nature:** incarnate in Jesus of Nazareth; suffering and resurrected; moral judge; Spirit-active.
- **Testable hooks:** [TH] all historical claims about Jesus (§3.1: crucifixion consensus high; resurrection contested); [TP] claimed miracles and answered prayer (§3.2); [TH] New Testament textual history (§3.1).
- **Non-testable core:** hypostatic union, atonement metaphysics, sacramental grace — all stated so as to be beyond test [NT].

### 2.4 Islam
- **Number:** one (tawhid), strictly no incarnation (Q 112:1–4: "He begets not, nor is He begotten").
- **Nature:** Creator, Merciful, Judge; Quran as uncreated speech of God revealed to Muhammad (d. 632 CE).
- **Testable hooks:** [TH] manuscript dating/preservation claims (Sana'a, Birmingham — §3.1); [TP] splitting of the moon (Q 54:1) — no astronomical record of a lunar cleavage event, the medieval/celestial record being silent [TP, H]; [TP] embryology "scientific foreknowledge" claims (Q 23:12–14) — description parallels 2nd-c. CE Galen, known in late-antique Syria [TH/H, contested]; [TH] Byzantine-victory prediction (Q 30:2–3) — consistent with Heraclius' 628 CE victory over Persia, composition dates muddied by periods of sura-dating [TH, contested M].
- **Non-testable core:** God's essence and attributes (*Asma' al-Husna*: 99 names as attributes are documentary; the attributes themselves NT); revelation's authority.

### 2.5 Hindu theism (Vaishnavism / Shaivism / Shaktism; smartism)
- **Number:** many gods, all forms/manifestations of one ultimate divine reality (Ishvara/Brahman in personal mode); typically "33 koti deities" (classical count; numerically ambiguous in sources) [M].
- **Nature/attributes:** cyclic time; avatars (Krishna as *purnavatar*, Bhagavad Gita ch. 4, 7, 9–10); world as divine play (lila); deity-presence in murti (icons).
- **Testable hooks:** [TP] Rama's bridge/Adam's Bridge — a real ~30–48 km chain of shoals; geological consensus: tombolo; NASA publicly disavowed mythological readings (2007) §3.2; [TH] Dwarka marine archaeology claims (NIOT; contested); [TH] Mahabharata chronology vs. archaeology (contested).
- **Non-testable core:** avatar ontology, murti-presence, lila [NT].

### 2.6 Advaita Vedanta (non-dualism)
- **Number:** not zero-one-many; Brahman is non-dual, beyond count.
- **Nature:** Brahman = pure consciousness, beyond attributes; the world as maya/appearance; atman = Brahman ("thou art that," Chandogya 6). **Does not posit a personal creator god in the Abrahamic sense** (established ground truth; consistent with the brief). Shankara (8th c. CE); the method is *neti neti* ("not this, not this," Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 2.3.6; 4.5.1).
- **Testable hooks:** [TH] text-dating (Vedic corpus closed well before Shankara; classical Upanishads c. 800–200 BCE); [SP] soteriological claims (moksha-experience reports — subjective/neurological, §3.2).
- **Non-testable core:** virtually the entire metaphysical content [NT] — deliberately so (apophatic).

### 2.7 Dharmic non-theistic systems: Jainism, Buddhism; Sikhism as non-Abrahamic monotheism
- **Jainism [NT-core]:** no creator; universe uncreated and eternal; souls (jiva) trapped by karma; tirthankaras liberate but do not create (Tattvartha Sutra). Testable hooks: cosmology (Mount Meru at world-center, concentric continents — contradicted by modern geography [TP, contradicted]); ethical claims.
- **Buddhism [NT-core]:** beginningless samsara; conditioned arising; no creator deity (Brahmajala Sutta explicitly rejects creator doctrine); Nagarjuna's emptiness doctrine (MMK) is anti-essentialist by design [NT]. The Kalama Sutta (AN 3.65) is itself an *anti-testimony epistemology* ("do not go by tradition"). Testable hooks: [SP] reincarnation-memory cases (Stevenson corpus, §3.2); [SP] meditation-state neurology (§3.2).
- **Sikhism:** one God (*Ik Onkar*, Guru Nanak 1469–1539), formless (nirankar), creator, non-incarnate; historically testable: Guru lineage and canon formation (Guru Arjan compilation 1604; Guru Gobind Singh's investiture of the Adi Granth as Guru Granth Sahib, 1708) [TH, H].

### 2.8 Ancient Near Eastern: Mesopotamia, Canaan, Zoroastrianism
- **Mesopotamian [TH-heavy]:** Marduk (Enuma Elish) creates from Tiamat's body after divine battle; gods are anthropomorphic, mortal-ish, morally ambiguous; polytheistic.
- **Canaanite [TH]:** El + Asherah (poetic pairing in Ugaritic texts, Ras Shamra, 12th–13th c. BCE manuscripts of older material); Baal the storm/fertility warrior; dead-and-rising motif of Baal.
- **Zoroastrianism:** Ahura Mazda — uncreated, all-good creator opposed by Angra Mainyu (dualistic); Zurvanite variant: both are twins of Zurvan (fate). No omnibenevolence (cosmic struggle), no incarnation. Avestan Gathas attributed to Zarathustra, dated anywhere c. 1500–1000 BCE, mainstream ≈1200–1000 BCE [TH, contested].
- **All:** deity-existence claims are [NT]; pantheon composition is [TH] (inscriptions) and largely established.

### 2.9 Greco-Roman
- **Olympian gods:** immortal, anthropomorphic, not omnipotent, not omnibenevolent, not creators of matter; Zeus is strongest, not absolute (Greek theogonies; Hesiod).
- **Philosophical remixes:** Plato's *Timaeus* demiurge (craftsman, morally good but not omnipotent, orders pre-existing stuff); Aristotle's Unmoved Mover (Metaphysics Λ.6–10: pure thought thinking itself, "God," cause as final cause, not personal, not creator in time).
- **Testable hooks:** [TH] cult sites, oracles, ritual calendars, priest lists (established); [TP] miracle/curse claims (mostly oral); [TH] theologia/mythera reconcilers (Palaephatus).

### 2.10 Deism
- **Nature:** a creator/first-cause who does not intervene; no miracles, no revelation; discoverable by reason from design.
- **Testability:** the intervention-denial *shields* the claim from miracle-tests (no miracles predicted) while the design claim is probabilistic (§3.3). Classical statement: Collins, Wollaston; American deism (Paine, *The Age of Reason*, 1794). **Flew's 2004 conversion was *only* to deism** — the live falsifier of interventionist claims (miracles) was conceded, and nothing more won; an instructive boundary-case [M].

### 2.11 Pantheism (Spinoza, *Ethics*, 1677)
- **Number/nature:** one substance, *Deus sive Natura*; God = Nature = all that is; no personal will, no mind in the human sense, no freedom; "intellectual love of God" as the highest good.
- **Testability:** substantive pantheism's existence-claim is not empirically separable from naturalism: both predict everything the world actually does [NT by L3]. As a redefinition of "God," it converts a metaphysical question into a semantic one.

### 2.12 Panentheism / process theism (Whitehead, *Process and Reality*, 1929; Hartshorne; dipolar theism; Kabbalistic *tzimtzum* variety)
- **Nature:** world in God; God has two poles — abstract necessity + consequent experience; God is not omnipotent in the classical sense (persuasion, not coercion).
- **Testability:** process metaphysics predicts nothing over classical theism [NT]; its strongest testable hook is precisely the problem of evil/animal-suffering scoring (it accepts non-omnipotence — see §1, L5b).

### 2.13 Progressive-revelation systems (Baha'i)
- **Nature:** God unknowable in essence; knowable through Manifestations (Abraham, Krishna, Zoroaster, Buddha, Jesus, Muhammad, the Bab, Baha'u'llah); progressive dispensations.
- **Testable hooks:** [TH] recorded dated prophecies (e.g., announcements concerning Napoleon III in the *Súriy-i-Haykal*, 1868–69 and divine trials texts; successes/failures debated by scholars — contested M); institutional history [TH, H].
- **Non-testable core:** unknowability itself.

## 4. What would count as evidence, class by class (brief item 3)

**Minimal admissibility rule (call it M0):** for any claim C to be adjudicable at all, there must exist a describable state of affairs E such that P(E | C) ≫ P(E | best rivals) — i.e., the claim must change the expected evidence. Anything that changes nothing (L2) or changes it identically to a rival (L3) is not adjudicable.

| class | example claim | what would count as evidence FOR | what would count as AGAINST | verdict of this audit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Existential/numerical ("exactly one God" / "no God") | classical theism's numerical claim | none available: "one God" is not an observable count; possible-worlds talk is modal, not empirical | same | **Not testable in principle at this level of individuation** |
| B. Attribute claims (omnipotence, omniscience, benevolence, simplicity) | "God is omnibenevolent" | none, as usually stated: attributes are stipulated or defined-to-fit (e.g., omnipotence-with-logical-limits) | internal inconsistency arguments (problem of evil) are evidential-argumentative, not tests | **Mostly not testable**; the evil problem is an argument, not an experiment — see L5b, §1 |
| C. Action claims (miracles, answered prayer, prophecy) | "prayer heals," "this prophecy was fulfilled," "this statue wept" | the B-protocols (§3.2): pre-registered, controlled, replicated, physically instrumented, examined for base-rates (spontaneous remission) and fraud | null results under the same protocol | **Testable in principle**: results to date — mixed, with best-controlled trials null (B2, B10, B11) plus unresolved anomalies (B5, B8, B17-features) that are, notably, **non-specific** (B6, B18) |
| D. Textual/philological claims | preservation, dating, authorship, "foreknowledge" of later science | manuscript carbon dates, early attestation, citation-chains, source-critical dating | variants, later composition dates, dependence on earlier sources | **Testable; largely resolved** (§3.1): textual claims resolved; "prophecy" almost always reduces to late composition or vaticinium ex eventu |
| E. Phenomenological/soteriological claims | "mystical experience is veridical contact with God"; reincarnation memories | phenomenological reports correlate with consistent, culture-independent facts | reports track prior culture (B13), or neurostimulation reproduces them (B12) | **Weakly testable**; evidence indicates shaping by prior belief, but "shaped experience" is logically independent of "false experience" — a residual core remains untestable |
| F. Hiddenness itself | "a loving God would ensure non-resistant belief" | the demographic distribution (§1 L6) is the data | CORNEA-style strategies make any distribution compatible | **Testable as sociology; unfalsifiable as theology** — the immunization move |

**Why no evidence in classes C–E can adjudicate the core commitments (the theorem of this artifact):**
1. Every well-behaved C/E test, when it succeeds, confirms only a *mundine substrate* (a manuscript, a shoal, a remission's timing, a testimony's earliness). Inferring "and therefore the metaphysics of tradition T" requires the extra premise that T's metaphysics is the best explanation — which is precisely what the parity argument (L3) forbids.
2. Every well-behaved C/E test that produces a null or a failure (B2, B10, B15) fails only the *operationalized* claim ("God answers measurable prayer"), not the metaphysical core — theology can retreat (L2) to unfalsifiable form without logical cost. **Failure does not reach the core; success does not discriminate among cores.**
3. The apophatic cores (Ein Sof, "beyond being," "neti neti," divine simplicity) are *designed* to be consequence-free: they sit outside the space of possible evidence by construction. (This is the theistic sibling of Sagan's dragon — a claim that no observation can touch.)
4. Hiddenness arguments can always be leveled symmetrically against any tradition (why did *this* God remain hidden to the people who followed a *rival*?), so hiddenness never selects among live options — it at most quantifies the spread.

Conclusion of §4, stated as a structural claim and not a verdict: **the evidential equilibrium is a tie at the level of the core commitments.** That equilibrium is a claim about *evidence*, not about *truth*: it is compatible with there being a God, with there being none, and with the question being ill-posed (L1).

## 5. Why "proof" is the wrong category (brief item 4)

Deductive proof requires (i) premises whose truth is not in dispute, (ii) valid inference, (iii) a conclusion that is not question-begging. The candidates' failure modes:

1. **Ontological proofs** trade on axioms that encode their conclusion (S5 + positive properties; existence-as-perfection). Gödel's version being machine-verified (Benzmüller & Paleo 2013/2014) shows the *derivation* is valid; it does not show the *premises* are more probable than their negations.
2. **Cosmological proofs** trade on the PSR, whose scope is the contested point, plus the modal assumption that the terminus of explanation is a God.
3. **Design/fine-tuning** are probabilistic: priors (multiverse, selection, brains) remain free; a posterior computation cannot outrun its prior.
4. **Bayesian theologies** (Swinburne) concede the same point: they raise probability, they do not decide.
5. **Evidential trials of miracles** (C/E) will, as shown, either be underdetermined (parity) or immunized (hiddenness/CORNEA) when results are unwelcome.
6. **The "many proofs" problem**: what any tradition offers as "evidence" (testimony, antiquity, personal experience, textual coherence) is equally offered by incompatible traditions (B18). Testimony conflict is not proof; it is a symmetry.

Final structural statement: **"Proof" belongs to the space of claims that carry truth-conditions that evidence can move. A claim with no discriminating content can be *asserted* with conviction but cannot be *proven*; a claim devoid of content can be neither proven nor disproven. The discipline-appropriate output for such claims is therefore clarification — the question "what would count as proof, and what exactly is being claimed?" — which is what this artifact attempts.**

**Established (this artifact's claims, all of which are structural, not verdicts):**
1. A 14-class taxonomy of divine conceptions with precise attribute-maps of each: number, nature/attributes, relation to world, and its canonical textual/historical anchors (§2).
2. A six-type classification of every kind of claim found in those traditions (§4, classes A–F).
3. An evidence ledger of 37 auditable claim-rows with current results and confidence ratings (§3): mundane confirmations (A1, A4–A8, A11, A17-feature), failed dated predictions (A12 tail, A13 literal reading, B15, B16), best-controlled nulls for action claims (B2, B10, B11), unresolved-but-non-specific anomalies (B5, B8).
4. A specification of what would count as proof for each class, and the parity/immunization reasons no class reaches the core commitments (§4–§5).
5. An operational account of the five ways a claim could become decidable (below).

**Remaining unknown / unresolved (honest gaps):**
- The precise population-level NDE percentages (B11 sub-figures vary by denominator across the AWARE reports) and AWARE II's final results — needs the live literature.
- Exact Lourdes claim-count and "7,000" figure (source variation); Fatima crowd-count (30k–100k range across reporters).
- The Zion of §2: several dated-prophecy claims (Baha'i/Napoleon III; Qur'an 30:2–3) need philological check before firm classification.
- Whether any proposed evidential discriminator exists that I have never seen — that is a permanent unknown by design of the parity problem.

**What would change my mind about the structure of the problem (definite feasibility conditions):**
1. **A decisive, pre-registered, replicated action-claim result with a theological signature** — e.g., a healing meeting an independent medical standard in a condition-controlled trial where (a) controls fail appropriately, (b) the effect occurs only for the hypothesized tradition's deity, solving B18's specificity deficit. That would not establish the metaphysics directly, but it would break the C/E parity and force a re-analysis.
2. **A resolution of the parity problem**: any evidential asymmetry that genuinely favors one conception without presupposing its truth (a hard, possibly impossible, condition — stating it is the honest form of the demand).
3. **A testimony-chronology dating of a sacred text before its purported predictions**, taken neutrally from external chronicles — the single most informative historical test (would convert A12-style reductions).
4. **An operationalization theorem**: a demonstration that an apophatic core can be cashed out in E-talk without immunization — moving the question from metaphysics into physics of mind/cosmology, where §4 rules would then apply.
5. **Symmetric condition**: a demonstration that naturalism or any particular metaphysics has a predicting core no rival shares — which would make the same rules apply to it.
