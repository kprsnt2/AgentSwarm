## 3. The BBN consistency triangle (the logic)

Standard BBN has exactly one free cosmological parameter, the baryon-to-photon
ratio η. Deuterium is the most sensitive abundance, so D/H *measures* η; with that
η, BBN then **predicts** ⁴He and ⁷Li with no further freedom. This yields a
three-way, parameter-free consistency test of the hot-dense state at T ≈ 1 MeV:

```
D/H  --->  eta  --->  Y_p   (4He)
                 \-->  7Li/H
```

If the hot, dense, radiation-dominated state at T ~ 1 MeV is correctly described,
all three predicted abundances must match the observed primordial values.

## 4. Step 1 — η from deuterium and from the CMB (independent, agree)

| Route | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| η from D/H (BBN) | η₁₀ = 6.10 ± 0.04 | Cooke et al. 2018, ApJ 855, 102 |
| η from CMB (100 Ω_b h² = 2.237 ± 0.015) | η₁₀ = 6.114 ± 0.041 | Planck 2018 VI, A&A 641, A6 |
| **Difference** | **Δ = 0.014 ± 0.057 → 0.24σ** | arithmetic (this work) |

The baryon density inferred at T ~ 1 MeV (BBN, from D/H) and at T ~ 0.3 eV
(recombination, from the CMB) agree to **0.24σ** — i.e. the hot-dense state is
empirically the *same* state across ~9.6 decades of temperature. This is a strong,
quantitative confirmation of the ratified clause.

## 5. Step 2 — the three predicted abundances vs observation

Adopting η₁₀ = 6.10 and the Pitrou et al. (2018) recommended rates:

| Element | BBN prediction | Observed primordial | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| D/H | 2.55 × 10⁻⁵ | (2.547 ± 0.025) × 10⁻⁵ | ✅ anchored (input; <0.1%) |
| Y_p (⁴He) | 0.2471 ± 0.0002 | 0.2449 ± 0.0040 (Aver+2015) | ✅ 0.55σ |
| Y_p (⁴He) | 0.2471 ± 0.0002 | 0.2551 ± 0.0022 (Izotov+2014) | ⚠️ 3.6σ (group tension) |
| ⁷Li/H | 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ | 1.58 × 10⁻¹⁰ (Sbordone+2010) | ❌ **factor 3.54 high** |

**Helium caveat (honest).** The two leading observational Y_p values differ from
each other by 2.2σ, so the helium test is limited by an unresolved inter-group
systematic (~0.005–0.01 in Y_p), not by BBN. BBN's 0.2471 lies between them and is
0.55σ from Aver et al. and 3.6σ from Izotov et al. Within the observational spread,
helium is consistent — but it is not a clean sub-percent confirmation.

## 6. Step 3 — the lithium problem, quantified

- Predicted ⁷Li/H = 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ (Pitrou et al. 2018, Phys. Rep. 754, 1).
- Observed Spite-plateau value: A(Li) = 2.199 ± 0.086 → ⁷Li/H = 1.58 × 10⁻¹⁰
  (Sbordone et al. 2010, A&A 522, A26).
- **Ratio = 3.54 (0.550 dex).**
- Significance: with a conservative theoretical/nuclear uncertainty of 0.10–0.20 dex
  and 0.086 dex observational scatter, the discrepancy is **2.5σ–4.2σ**
  (4.2σ at 0.10 dex, 3.2σ at 0.15 dex, 2.5σ at 0.20 dex). It is therefore a
  *robust but systematics-limited* anomaly, not a decisive statistical rejection.

**Direction matters.** The prediction is *higher* than the plateau. The leading
astrophysical escape is therefore stellar ⁷Li depletion: if the plateau stars have
destroyed a factor ~3.5 of their initial lithium (via rotational mixing or internal
gravity waves), the true primordial value could be ~5.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ and BBN would be
vindicated. Uniform depletion of this size is plausible in models but is not
independently confirmed.

## 7. What this does — and does not — do to the ratified statement

- **Does not falsify** "hot, dense state": D/H and (within systematics) ⁴He anchor
  the T ~ 1 MeV state to the same baryon density as the CMB. Two of three BBN
  predictions succeed.
- **Does** show the statement's BBN foundation is not fully closed: one of its three
  quantitative pillars, ⁷Li, is off by a factor ~3.5. The ratified statement does not
  mention this; that is a gap in the *statement*, not an error in its quoted values.
- The statement's Y_p = 0.247 is the BBN central value and is consistent with the
  deuterium-calibrated η; it is not the number most in tension. The tension is in
  lithium, which the statement omits.

## 8. Established / Unknown / What would change my mind

- **Established (this turn, arithmetic on published data):** the CMB and BBN baryon
  densities agree at 0.24σ (η₁₀ = 6.10–6.11); D/H and Y_p confirm the hot-dense
  state; ⁷Li is predicted 3.54× too high (0.55 dex, ~2.5–4σ), the one quantitative
  failure among the three BBN abundances.
- **Unknown (unchanged):** whether the Spite plateau is depleted by ~0.55 dex;
  whether a nuclear rate (³He(α,γ)⁷Be, ⁷Be(d,p)⁸B, ⁷Li(p,α)⁴He) is wrong at the
  required level; whether new physics (decaying particles) is needed; the correct
  H0; the nature of dark matter; the initial singularity.
- **What would change my mind:** (a) a measured ⁷Li depletion factor in plateau
  stars of ~3.5 with an independent abundance indicator (would move primordial ⁷Li
  to the BBN value and dissolve the problem astrophysically); (b) a verified
  factor-~3 increase in the ³He(α,γ)⁷Be rate (currently constrained at the ~10%
  level by experiment, so this is unlikely); (c) a confirmed non-standard
  energy-injection mechanism that destroys ⁷Be while leaving D and ⁴He intact.
  Absent one of these, the lithium discrepancy stands as an open quantitative
  weakness of the ratified statement's BBN foundation.
