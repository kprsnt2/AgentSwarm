## Part 1: Deep Literature Analysis (What is Known vs. What is NOT Known)

### 1.1 What is Known in Postnatal Cardiac Biology

1. **The Postnatal Day 7 (P7) Regenerative Window:**  
   In 2011, Porrello et al. demonstrated that neonatal mice can fully regenerate their myocardium following surgical ventricular apex resection if performed at Postnatal Day 1 (P1). By Postnatal Day 7 (P7), this regenerative capacity is abruptly and permanently lost. A similar transient neonatal regenerative capacity was subsequently documented in human newborns with perinatal myocardial infarction.
2. **The Postnatal Switch from Hyperplasia to Hypertrophy:**  
   During fetal development, cardiac growth occurs predominantly via **hyperplasia** (mononucleated cardiomyocytes proliferating and undergoing complete cytokinesis). In the first week after birth in rodents (and within the first 6–12 months in humans), circulating thyroid hormone surges and oxygen-rich atmospheric air prompts a metabolic shift from anaerobic glycolysis to fatty acid $\beta$-oxidation. Cardiomyocytes exit the cell cycle, uncouple karyokinesis from cytokinesis, and undergo a final round of DNA replication without division, generating a population of **binucleated polyploid cells** (representing ~85% of adult rodent and ~30–40% of adult human myocytes). Subsequent organ growth is driven exclusively through **hypertrophy** (enlargement of individual cell volumes).
3. **The Role of the Hippo-YAP Pathway:**  
   The evolutionarily conserved Hippo kinase cascade—comprising MST1/2 kinases, SAV1 scaffold protein, and LATS1/2 kinases—is a primary negative regulator of organ size. When Hippo signaling is active (the default state in dense adult myocardium), LATS1/2 directly phosphorylates the transcriptional co-activator **YAP** at Ser127 (and TAZ at Ser89). Phosphorylated YAP is sequestered in the cytoplasm by binding to 14-3-3 proteins and subsequently degraded by the $\beta$-TrCP ubiquitin-proteasome system. Genetic deletion of Hippo components (*Sav1* or *Lats1/2*) or expression of a constitutively active YAP mutant (*YAP5SA* or *YAP-S127A*) induces adult cardiomyocyte cell-cycle re-entry.
4. **The Energetic and Oxidative Checkpoint:**  
   Mitochondrial metabolism in adult cardiomyocytes produces substantial reactive oxygen species (ROS), which trigger a sustained DNA damage response (DDR) mediated by ATM/ATR kinases and p53/p21, enforcing permanent cell-cycle arrest (Puente et al., 2014). Placing animals in a severe hypoxic environment (7% $O_2$) reduces oxidative DNA damage and reactivates a low baseline level of cardiomyocyte division.

## Part 2: Biophysical & Molecular Architecture of the Cleavage Furrow

### 2.1 The Mechanical Cleavage-Tension Threshold Theorem

The cytokinesis cleavage furrow is modeled as an axisymmetric constriction neck undergoing contractile closing against internal myofibrillar resistance.

Let:
- $r$: Furrow radius at the constriction neck ($\mu\text{m}$).
- $T_{\text{ring}}$: Actomyosin contractile ring line tension ($\text{nN}/\mu\text{m}$).
- $T_{\text{membrane}}$: Passive plasma membrane cortical tension ($\text{nN}/\mu\text{m}$).
- $F_{\text{sarcomere}}$: Passive resisting force exerted by the internal sarcomeric and titin cytoskeleton ($\text{nN}$).

The net inward constricting force $F_{\text{net}}(r)$ is governed by the force balance equation:

$$F_{\text{net}}(r) = 2\pi r \cdot \left(T_{\text{ring}} - T_{\text{membrane}}\right) - F_{\text{sarcomere}}$$

For cytokinesis to proceed toward the critical midbody diameter required for ESCRT-III abscission ($r \le r_{\text{abscission}} = 0.60\,\mu\text{m}$), the condition must be satisfied:

$$F_{\text{net}}(r) > 0 \quad \forall \; r \in [r_{\text{abscission}}, r_{\text{initial}}]$$

#### The Biophysical Breakdown in Adult Cardiomyocytes:
1. In adult resting cardiomyocytes:
   - $T_{\text{ring}} = 3.20\,\text{nN}/\mu\text{m}$
   - $T_{\text{membrane}} = 0.45\,\text{nN}/\mu\text{m}$
   - $(T_{\text{ring}} - T_{\text{membrane}}) = 2.75\,\text{nN}/\mu\text{m}$
   - $F_{\text{sarcomere}} = 14.20\,\text{nN}$ (due to dense $\alpha$-actinin $Z$-discs, nebulin, and immunoglobulin-like domains of PEVK-titin).
2. At the critical bottleneck radius $r = 0.60\,\mu\text{m}$:

$$F_{\text{constriction}} = 2\pi \cdot 0.60\,\mu\text{m} \cdot 2.75\,\text{nN}/\mu\text{m} = 10.367\,\text{nN}$$

$$F_{\text{net}} = 10.367\,\text{nN} - 14.20\,\text{nN} = -3.833\,\text{nN}$$

Because $F_{\text{net}} < 0$, the cleavage furrow stalls at $r \approx 0.82\,\mu\text{m}$. The actomyosin ring cannot overcome the passive mechanical resistance of the sarcomere. Sustained isometric tension results in contractile ring disassembly, furrow regression, and stable binucleation.
