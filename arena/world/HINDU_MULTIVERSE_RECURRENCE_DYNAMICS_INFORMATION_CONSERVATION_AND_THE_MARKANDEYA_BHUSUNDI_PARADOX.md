### 2.1 Sanskrit Philological Foundations

> **स मां दृष्ट्वा महाराज प्रहसन्निव वारिजः । आचचक्षे महातेजा मामेवेह सुविस्मितम् ॥**  
> **अथाहं तेन बालेन सहसा विवृताननः । प्रक्षिप्तो वदने तस्य दैवेन प्रहितो यथा ॥**  
> **तस्य बाहुल्ये विस्तीर्णे कुक्षौ राजन्महात्मनः । समग्रमन्वपश्यं च जगत्स्थावरजङ्गमम् ॥**

> *"Seeing me, O Great King, that radiant child, smiling, looked upon me as I stood astounded. Suddenly opening his mouth, I was drawn inside by divine power. Inside the vast, expansive belly (*kukṣau*) of that Great Being, O King, I beheld the entire universe, both moving and unmoving."*

> **न चान्तमधिगच्छामि तस्य कुक्षौ विचेष्टतः । ततो बालेन महता मुक्तेन सहसोच्छ्वसन् ॥**

Then, as the great child exhaled, I was suddenly expelled outward."*

### 3.1 The Strict Identical Recurrence School (*Nitya-Sadṛśa-Sṛṣṭi-Vāda*)

The classical Vedic and Vedāntic orthodoxy asserts strict cyclical invariance based on the foundational cosmogonic hymn *Ṛgveda* 10.190.3:

> **सूर्याचन्द्रमसौ धाता यथापूर्वमकल्पयत् । दिवं च पृथिवीं चान्तरिक्षमथो स्वः ॥ ३ ॥**  
> — *Ṛgveda* 10.190.3
>
> *"The Ordainer (*Dhātā*) fashioned the sun and the moon exactly as before (*yathā-pūrvam*), heaven and earth, the intermediate realm, and the domain of light."*

In his commentary on *Brahmasūtra* 1.3.30 (*samāna-nāmarūpatvāc ca viśeṣo 'pi sadṛśatvāt*), Śaṅkara explains why the cosmos must be an exact recurrence:

> *"Because the names and forms in the new creation are identical with those of the previous creations, there is no contradiction regarding the eternity of the Vedic words. When a cycle dissolves, the potencies (*śaktis*) of the universe remain latent in the causal state. At the dawn of the next cycle, the Creator reproduces the very same names and forms—Indra, Agni, Prajāpati, human social orders, and natural kinds—exactly as they existed previously."*

For Śaṅkara and the Mīmāṁsakas (Kumārila Bhaṭṭa), if history deviated arbitrarily, the Vedic injunctions (*vidhis*) would lose their eternal semantic reference (*saṅketa*). Therefore, the universe operates as a **strictly periodic deterministic loop**.

### 3.2 The Stochastic Permutation School (*Vilakṣaṇa-Sṛṣṭi-Vāda* / *Bhuśuṇḍopākhyāna*)

In radical contrast, the *Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha* (Nirvāṇa Prakaraṇa, Pūrvārdha, sargas 14–27) presents the testimony of the immortal crow Kākabhuśuṇḍi, who has survived hundreds of Mahāpralayas by remaining in the subtle breath (*Prāṇāyāma*) atop Mount Meru.

Bhuśuṇḍi reveals to Sage Vasiṣṭha that historical cycles are **not** identical replicas, but kaleidoscopic permutations:

> **द्वादशकृत्वो महीं मग्नामपश्यं वारिधौ मुने । दशकृत्वो वराहेण समुद्धृतामवेक्षितम् ॥**  
> **सप्तकृत्वोऽमृतार्थं च मथितं क्षीरसागरम् । एकादशकृत्वो रामस्य चरितं सुविचेष्टितम् ॥**  
> **षोडशकृत्वो भारतं युद्धं वृत्तं मया श्रुतम् । नानाजातिसमायुक्तं नानाव्यूहसमाकुलम् ॥**  
> — *Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha* 6.1.22.14–25
>
> *"Twelve times I have seen the earth submerged in the ocean, O sage. Ten times I watched her rescued by the Boar (Varāha). Seven times the Milk Ocean was churned for nectar. Eleven times the exploits of Rāma were enacted. Sixteen times I witnessed the Mahābhārata war, fought with diverse clans and varied battle formations."*

## 4. Trans-Pralayic Thermodynamics & Cosmological Information Conservation

In modern theoretical physics, the black hole information paradox centers on whether microscopic information is lost when matter collapses into an event horizon. In Indian philosophy, an analogous problem arose regarding cosmic dissolution: the **Cosmological Information Paradox (*Pralaya-Saṁskāra-Saṅkaṭa*)**.

```
                 Δ I_karma = 0 (Strict Unitary Conservation).

                 Zero souls destroyed; zero unmerited rewards (Akṛtābhyāgama = False).
```

### 4.1 The Thermodynamic Crisis of Pralaya

In classical Sāṅkhya philosophy (*Sāṅkhya-Kārikā* 16), during universal dissolution, the three fundamental qualities of primordial nature (*Prakṛti*)—Sattva (harmony/luminosity), Rajas (dynamism/passion), and Tamas (inertia/darkness)—settle into a state of perfect balance:

$$\text{Guṇa-Sāmyāvasthā} \iff \text{Homogeneous Transformation } (Sadṛśa-Pariṇāma)$$

If the three Guṇas are in perfect equilibrium, all differentiated motion ceases. This superficially resembles thermodynamic heat death:

$$\Delta S_{\text{universe}} > 0, \quad S \to S_{\text{max}}$$

However, classical Indian thought identified a severe logical danger: **If all differentiation is destroyed, what causes the next creation to arise?** And more fundamentally, **where do the karmic accounts of living beings go?**

### 4.2 The Moral Necessity of Information Conservation

If karmic information were erased during Pralaya, the two pillars of Indian soteriological ethics would collapse:

1. **Kṛta-Vipraṇāśa (Destruction of the Earned):** A person who performed virtuous deeds would be cheated of their karmic fruit because dissolution wiped the slate clean.
2. **Akṛtābhyāgama (Arrival of the Unearned):** When the next universe is created, beings born into happiness or suffering would receive these states without any prior causal justification, making God unjust or whimsical (*vaiṣamya-nairghṛṇya*).

To prevent this catastrophe, Vedānta formulated the **Strict Unitary Conservation of Karmic Information**:

$$\Delta I_{\text{karma}} = 0 \quad (\text{across any finite or infinite sequence of Pralayas})$$

### 4.3 Rāmānuja’s Formulation (*Śrībhāṣya* 1.1.1 & *Vedārthasaṅgraha* 65)

In Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta, Rāmānuja resolves the paradox by distinguishing two ontological states of the Supreme Reality:

- **Kāryāvasthā (The Effect-State):** Brahman embodied by conscious souls (*cit*) and unconscious matter (*acit*) in their differentiated, gross, manifest names and forms (*nāma-rūpa-vyākṛta*).
- **Kāraṇāvasthā (The Causal-State):** Brahman embodied by *cit* and *acit* in their extremely subtle, unmanifest, undifferentiated condition (*sūkṣma-cit-acit-prakāra*).

Rāmānuja writes in *Śrībhāṣya* (1.1.1):

> *"During the period of universal dissolution, the individual souls remain in a subtle condition, deprived of physical bodies and sense-organs, with their intelligence contracted (*saṅkucita-jñāna*), but retaining their individual latent karmic impressions (*saṁskāras*). They remain inseparably attached (*apṛthak-siddhi*) to the Lord as His subtle body. When creation occurs, the Lord expands them according to their prior karma (*yathā-karma*), providing them with appropriate bodies."*

Thus, in Hindu cosmology, **information is never lost**. The universe transitions between expanded and contracted phases with zero entropy loss in the moral-informational register.
