### Section 3: Textual Stratigraphy and the Epigraphic Deification Trajectory

The Mahābhārata Critical Edition (BORI, edited by V.S. Sukthankar et al., 1933–1959) analyzed 1,259 manuscripts across multiple scripts, establishing that the epic is an accretive, multi-layered text.

#### The Three Structural Epic Strata:
1. **Jaya (Victory Ballad):** $\approx 8,800$ verses (attested in MBh 1.1.81: *aṣṭau śloka-sahasrāṇi aṣṭau śloka-śatāni ca*). Focuses on the military tactical conflict between the Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas. Krishna appears primarily as a mortal ally, diplomat, and counselor.
2. **Bhārata:** $\approx 24,000$ verses (MBh 1.1.61: *caturviṁśati-sāhasrīṁ cakre bhārata-saṁhitām*). Expands the dynastic genealogy, heroic codes, and ethical debates. Krishna is elevated to a legendary hero and divine-human teacher.
3. **Mahābhārata (Śatasahasrī Saṁhitā):** $\approx 100,000$ verses (attested in Gupta-era inscriptions c. 450 CE). Incorporates vast encyclopedic treatises (*Śāntiparvan*, *Anuśāsanaparvan*) and the *Harivaṁśa* appendix. Krishna is fully established as the supreme cosmic *Svayam Bhagavān* and universal soul (*Viśvarūpa*).

#### Mathematical Accretion Kinetics:
- Time span of transmission: c. 900 BCE (*Jaya*) to c. 400 CE (*Final Mahābhārata*) $\approx 1,300$ years.
- Accretion multiplier: $100,000 / 8,800 \approx 11.36\times$.
- Compound annual growth rate:
  $$r = (11.36)^{1/1300} - 1 \approx 0.187\% \text{ per year}$$
- Verse doubling time:
  $$T_{\text{double}} = \frac{\ln(2)}{\ln(1 + 0.001871)} \approx 370.8 \text{ years}$$

1. **c. 650 BCE – Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.17.6:**
   *tad haitad ghora āṅgirasaḥ kṛṣṇāya devakīputrāyoktvovāca...*  
   Earliest textual mention. Krishna is explicitly described as a human seeker, the son of Devakī (*devakīputra*), receiving spiritual instruction on non-violence (*ahiṁsā*) and inner sacrifice from sage Ghora Āṅgirasa.
2. **c. 450 BCE – Pāṇini’s Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.3.98:**
   *vāsudevārjunābhyāṁ vun*  
   Provides grammatical rules for forming words denoting religious devotion/veneration (*bhakti*) toward Vāsudeva and Arjuna, demonstrating that both figures had attained heroic/cultic veneration in the northwest by the 5th century BCE.
3. **c. 300 BCE – Megasthenes’ Indica (preserved in Arrian, Indica 8.5):**
   Records that the Indian tribe of the *Sourasenoi* (Śūrasenas) who possess two great cities, *Methora* (Mathura) and *Kleisobora* (Kṛṣṇapura/Vraja), along the navigable river *Jobares* (Yamunā), hold *Herakles* (the Greek *interpretatio* for Krishna-Vāsudeva) in extraordinary honor.
4. **c. 185 BCE – Bilingual Silver Coins of Indo-Greek King Agathocles (Ai-Khanoum, Bactria):**
   Discovered on the Oxus River. Obverse features Vāsudeva holding the *cakra* (discus) and *śaṅkha* (conch); reverse features Saṅkarṣaṇa (Balarāma) holding the *gadā* (mace) and plough, with Brahmi legend *Rājane Agathuklayasa*. This is the earliest known visual anthropomorphic depiction of Krishna-Vāsudeva.
5. **c. 113 BCE – Besnagar Pillar Inscription of Heliodoros (Vidisha):**
   *Devadevasa Vāsudevasa garuḍadhvaje ayaṁ kārite... Heliodoreṇa bhāgavatena...*  
   Erected by Heliodoros, son of Dion, an ambassador sent from the Indo-Greek King Antialcidas of Taxila to King Kasiputra Bhagabhadra of Vidisha. Heliodoros declares himself a *Bhāgavata* (worshipper of Vāsudeva) and dedicates a Garuḍa standard to Vāsudeva as *Devadeva* ("God of Gods").
6. **c. 50 BCE – Ghosundi and Hathibada Inscriptions (Nagari, Rajasthan):**
   Records the construction of a stone worship wall (*pūjā-śilā-prākāra*) in the Nārāyaṇa compound (*nārāyaṇa-vāṭikā*) for the joint worship of the Bhagavan brothers Saṅkarṣaṇa and Vāsudeva.
7. **c. 15 CE – Mora Well Inscription (Mathura, reign of Mahākṣatrapa Śoḍāsa):**
   *bhagavatāṁ vṛṣṇīnāṁ pañcavīrāṇāṁ pratimāḥ śailāmayyaḥ...*  
   Records the installation of stone images of the **Five Vṛṣṇi Heroes** (*Pañcavīrāḥ*): Saṅkarṣaṇa, Vāsudeva, Pradyumna, Śāmba, and Aniruddha.
   *Historiographical significance:* This inscription provides undeniable material proof that Vāsudeva Krishna was venerated alongside his brother, sons, and grandson as mortal deified clan heroes (*vīrāḥ*) before his complete theological subsumption into the transcendent cosmic Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa.
