## 6. Textual Growth Kinetics & The BORI Critical Edition

The Mahabharata was not composed in a single moment by an omniscient observer; it grew organically over centuries through bardic transmission, heroic performance, and philosophical interpolation.

### 6.1 The Three Stratigraphic Stages of Composition
The epic itself explicitly acknowledges its layered growth (*Adi Parva* 1.1.99–102):
1. **Stage 1: Jaya (~8,800 verses, c. 950–800 BCE):** The original heroic bardic ballad composed by Krishna Dvaipayana Vyasa and recited by court bards (*Sutas*), focusing strictly on the tragic 18-day civil war and the destruction of the Kuru clan.
2. **Stage 2: Bharata (~24,000 verses, c. 500–400 BCE):** Recited by Vaisampayana at the snake sacrifice of King Janamejaya; expanded to include the ancestral origins of the Bharatas, clan genealogies, and regional conflicts.
3. **Stage 3: Mahabharata (73,784 verses in BORI Critical Edition, finalized c. 4th century CE):** Recited by Ugrasravas Sauti to the rishis in the Naimisha forest; heavily redone by the Bhrigu and Angirasa priestly guilds (*Bhargava recension*), incorporating the entire encyclopedic framework of Dharma, cosmology, statecraft, the *Shanti* and *Anushasana* Parvas, and the Bhagavad Gita.
4. **Stage 4: The Vulgate / Nilakantha Recension (~100,000 verses, c. 17th century CE):** The late regional conflation containing post-classical interpolations, popular legends, and local scribal additions.
