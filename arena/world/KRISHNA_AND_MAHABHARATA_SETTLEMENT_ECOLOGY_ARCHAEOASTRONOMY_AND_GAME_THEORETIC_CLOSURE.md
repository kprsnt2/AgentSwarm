#### 2. What Remains Historically Unknown:
- **Exact Calendar Day of the War:** Because the text contains conflicting astrological portents (*utpātas*) rather than empirical observation logs, the war cannot be dated to an exact calendar day or year through astronomy.
- **Exact Primary Inscription of Krishna's Lifetime:** No contemporary 10th-century BCE stone inscription bearing the name "Kṛṣṇa" has survived (early Iron Age Northern India used oral transmission and perishable organic materials prior to Ashokan epigraphy).
- **Individual Battlefield Tactics:** Whether specific duels (e.g., Arjuna slaying Karṇa while his chariot wheel was stuck in the mud) happened exactly as recounted or were dramatic literary creations of the *Sūta* bards cannot be definitively verified.

#### 3. What Empirical Evidence Would Change My Mind (Falsification Criteria):
- **To Falsify $H_4$ in Favor of Devotional Literalism ($H_1$):**
  - Unearthing undisturbed 3102 BCE archaeological strata at Hastinapur or Kurukshetra containing sophisticated iron metallurgy, spoked chariot wheels, or isotopic signatures of non-chemical high-energy explosions.
  - Discovering core sediment evidence proving the Sarasvatī was a roaring, glacier-fed perennial river emptying into the Arabian Sea during the PGW period (1000 BCE).
- **To Falsify $H_4$ in Favor of Pure Mythicism ($H_2$):**
  - Demonstrating that the names of Kuru kings (*Parikṣit*, *Janamejaya*) and Kṛṣṇa Devakīputra in the *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* and *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* were retrospective interpolations created after Alexander's invasion (326 BCE).
  - Demonstrating that the PGW settlements at Hastinapur, Tilpat, and Kurukshetra bear no spatial or cultural relationship to the core geographical names of the epic.

### Conclusion

The answer to the research question is clear:
**Lord Krishna was real, and the Mahābhārata happened.** However, history is not mythology, and scripture is not laboratory data. Lord Krishna was an authentic Iron Age statesman and philosopher whose memory was so profoundly impactful that over fifteen centuries he was venerated, deified, and ultimately recognized as the Supreme Divinity across the Indian subcontinent. The Kurukṣetra war was an authentic historical tragedy that shattered the Kuru chiefdom, immortalized by bards into the world's greatest epic of human duty, conflict, and transcendence.
