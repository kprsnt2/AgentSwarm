Calling him *śiva* was an apotropaic liturgical invocation to propitiate his fury.
3. **Middle Vedic Horizon (c. 1000–800 BCE)**: In the *Śrī Rudram* (*Śatarudrīya*, Taittiriya Samhita 4.5), Rudra assimilates regional, pastoral, and indigenous forest divinities. The text famously identifies the divinity not only in kings and priests, but in carpenters, potters, hunters, forest-dwellers, and thieves (*taskarāṇāṁ patiḥ*), documenting a massive civilizational synthesis.
4. **Upanishadic Horizon (c. 600–300 BCE)**: The *Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* elevates Rudra-Shiva to the Supreme Brahman: *"Eko hi rudro na dvitīyāya tasthuḥ"* (3.2). He is defined as the Lord of Maya (*Māyinaṁ tu maheśvaram*, 4.10), bridging personal theism with non-dual metaphysical inquiry.

### 3.1 Domain 1: Hindu Puranic Sambhala-grāma (Physically Present)

In Hindu canonical literature, Shambhala is described not as an inaccessible Himalayan kingdom, but as a prominent agrarian town or village (*grāma*) located in northern India (*Āryāvarta*):

  > *śambhalagrāmamukhyasya brāhmaṇasya mahātmanaḥ /

#### Archaeological Stratigraphy of Sambhal (Uttar Pradesh):
The historical town of **Sambhal** is situated in Rohilkhand, Uttar Pradesh (**$28.58^\circ\text{ N}, 78.57^\circ\text{ E}$**). Continuous archaeological stratification establishes its physical antiquity:
1. **Proto-Historic & Early Iron Age (c. 1000–600 BCE)**: Painted Grey Ware (PGW) horizons in the upper Gangetic Doab.
2. **Early Historic Horizon (c. 600–100 BCE)**: Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW), terracotta artifacts, and punch-marked coins.
3. **Classical Puranic Horizon (c. 300–700 CE)**: Structural brick layers, Shaiva and Vaishnava iconography corresponding to the period when the *Vishnu* and *Bhagavata* Puranas reached their canonical forms.
4. **Medieval Rajput Citadel (c. 900–1192 CE)**: Historical seat of the Chauhan and Tomara rulers; venerated site of the *Hari Mandir* temple atop the highest citadel mound (*Kot*).
5. **Mughal Monumental Epigraphy (1526 CE)**: Following the First Battle of Panipat, Emperor Babur's commander, Mir Hindu Beg, constructed the **Baburi Jama Masjid of Sambhal** atop the central mound. The Persian inscriptional panel fixed into the central mihrab, dated **933 AH (December 1526 CE)**, verifies Babur's direct patronage at this ancient sacred site.

**Epistemic Verdict**: **PHYSICALLY PRESENT**. As an authentic geographic and archaeological settlement on Earth, the Puranic Shambhala is present today in Uttar Pradesh.
