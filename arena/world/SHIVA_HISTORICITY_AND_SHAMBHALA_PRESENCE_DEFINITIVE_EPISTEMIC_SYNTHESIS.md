### 3.1 Domain 1: The Hindu Puranic Tradition (*Śambhala-grāma*)

In the Hindu Puranas, Shambhala is described not as a mysterious hidden Himalayan kingdom, but as a specific village or agrarian town (*grāma*) located within Aryavarta (northern India).

#### Primary Textual Evidence
* **Bhāgavata Purāṇa (12.2.18)**:
  > *śambhalagrāmamukhyasya brāhmaṇasya mahātmanaḥ /  
  > bhavane viṣṇuyaśasaḥ kalkiḥ prādurbhaviṣyati //*  
  > *(In the house of the eminent brahmin of the village of Shambhala, the great-souled Vishnuyashas, Kalki will make his appearance.)*
* **Viṣṇu Purāṇa (4.24.98)**:
  > Confirms the birth of Kalki in the village of *Śambhala* to destroy adharma, inaugurate the Krita (Satya) Yuga, and restore Vedic order.
* **Mahābhārata (Vana Parva 188.89–91)**:
  > Contains the earliest epic prophecy of Kalki Vishnuyashas appearing in Sambhala.

#### Geographical and Historical Reality
* **Physical Identification**: The historic town of **Sambhal** located in the Moradabad division / Rohilkhand region of **Uttar Pradesh, India** (Coordinates: **28.58° N, 78.57° E**).
* **Archaeological & Archival Witness**:
  * Ancient settlement mounds dating back to the early historic period.
  * Continuously documented in Delhi Sultanate chronicles (e.g., *Tārīkh-i-Firishta*) and Mughal gazetteers (*Ā'īn-i-Akbarī*).
  * Babur visited Sambhal and commissioned the construction of the Jama Masjid of Sambhal in **1526 CE** (inscribed on the mosque walls), historically associated with an earlier Hindu temple site venerated as the sanctum of Kalki.
* **Epistemic Status**: **PHYSICALLY PRESENT**. As a geographic town in Uttar Pradesh, Shambhala (Sambhal) exists tangibly on Earth today.

### 3.2 Domain 2: The Buddhist Kalachakra Tantric Tradition

The most famous concept of Shambhala originates in the **Kālacakra Tantra** (*Laghu-kālacakra-tantra-rāja*) and its foundational commentary, the **Vimalaprabhā**, composed in northern India during the early 11th century CE (c. 1025–1040 CE).

1. **Topography**: Described as a circular realm north of the River Sita (historically identified with the Tarim River in Xinjiang or the Syr Darya / Jaxartes), ringed by an outer and inner barrier of snow mountains resembling an eight-petaled lotus flower.
2. **Administration**: Divided into 8 regions containing 96 principalities, surrounding the central capital city of **Kalāpa**, where the king rules from a palace of sandalwood and presides over a three-dimensional Kalachakra mandala.
3. **The Dynastic Lineage**:
   * **7 Dharmarājas** (beginning with King *Sucandra*, who according to tradition received the Kalachakra Mulatantra directly from Shakyamuni Buddha at Dhanyakataka).
   * **25 Kalkī Kings** (*Kulika* / Tibetan: *Rigden*), beginning with *Mañjuśrī-Yaśas* and *Puṇḍarīka*.
4. **The Eschatological Climax**:
   * In the reign of the 25th Kalki King, **Raudra Cakrin** (prophesied for approximately **2424 CE**, calculated as 400 years after the 1027 CE Tibetan sexagenary epoch), the barbarian invaders (*Mlecchas* / Tibetan: *Kla-klo*) will launch an invasion against Shambhala.
   * Raudra Cakrin will ride forth at the head of a supernatural army, defeat the barbarian hordes in an epic apocalyptic battle, and inaugurate an 800-year Golden Age of Dharma.

#### Historical-Critical Scholarly Consensus (Tucci, Hoffmann, Bernbaum, Newman)
1. **Historical Context of Composition (c. 1025 CE)**: The Kalachakra Tantra emerged during a catastrophic crisis in northern Indian Buddhism: the military raids of Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni (e.g., the sack of Somnath in 1026 CE and attacks on northwestern Buddhist/Hindu centers). The texts explicitly mention the religious doctrines and leaders of the invaders (identifying Adam, Moses, Jesus, Mani, Muhammad).
2. **Sociopolitical Myth & Utopia**: Shambhala functioned as an idealized socio-political sanctuary and eschatological promise, assuring beleaguered Indian Buddhists that their spiritual heritage was safely preserved in a northern superpower that would eventually restore righteous rule.
3. **Internal Yogic Allegory**: Crucially, the *Vimalaprabhā* commentary itself explicitly states that the apocalyptic external war is an **allegory for internal yogic psychophysical mastery**:

   * *The Barbarian Hordes (Kla-klo)* = The destructive mental afflictions (*kleśas*), ignorance, and dualistic conceptuality.
