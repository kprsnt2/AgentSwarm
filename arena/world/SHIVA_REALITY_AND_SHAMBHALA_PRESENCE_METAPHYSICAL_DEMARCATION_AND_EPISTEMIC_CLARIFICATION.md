### 2.1 Claim S1: Biological Mortal Human Euhemerism
* **What the Claim Asserts:** Lord Shiva was originally an ordinary biological mortal human being (e.g., an ancient tribal chieftain, king, or mortal yogi) who lived, aged, died, and was buried or cremated on Earth, and was subsequently exaggerated into a divinity through generations of folklore (euhemerism).
* **What Would Count as Evidence FOR It:**
  1. Contemporaneous bioarchaeological, epigraphic, or skeletal remains from the 3rd or 2nd millennium BCE documenting a mortal ruler named *Śiva* with mortal parents, regnal years, and cause of death.
  2. A verified tomb, reliquary, or burial mound with primary inscriptions establishing biological mortal genealogy.
* **What Would Count as Evidence AGAINST It:**
  1. Complete absence of mortal parents across all ancient textual corpora (consistently defined as *Anādi* [without beginning] and *Ayonija* [unborn from a womb]).
  2. Total absence of any mortal tomb, burial, or skeletal tradition across the Indian subcontinent.
  3. Continuous portrayal in the earliest strata (Rigveda, Yajurveda Śatarudrīya) as an elemental, atmospheric, and cosmic divine principle (*Rudra-Śiva*), not a mortal human.
* **Why It Does NOT Resist Testing:** This facet belongs to empirical history and bioarchaeology. It does not resist standard methods; the historical data simply do not match the mortal euhemeristic model.

$$\text{Category Error} = \text{Evaluating } \text{Pramātṛ} \text{ as if it were a } \text{Prameya}$$
