## What remains unknown (as stated in the consensus)
1. **Hubble tension** — 73.04 ± 1.04 vs 67.4 ± 0.5 km/s/Mpc is a ~5σ discrepancy.
   Not yet resolved by any confirmed systematic or new physics.
2. **Dark matter nature** — Ω_c h² = 0.120 ± 0.001 is well constrained
   gravitationally, but no confirmed non-gravitational detection exists.
3. **Initial singularity** — inflationary ΛCDM does not remove past-incompleteness;
   the Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem (PRL 90, 151301, 2003) shows a broad class of
   expanding cosmologies are past-incomplete. A quantum-gravity or bounce description
   is absent.

## Evidence that would change this ratification
- A confirmed dark-matter particle detection, or a demonstrated failure of the
  cold-dark-matter fit to structure/CMB data.
- Resolution of the Hubble tension to <1σ by an identified systematic or by new
  physics consistent with all other datasets.
- A non-singular, past-eternal cosmological model with testable CMB/B-mode signatures
  that fits current data at least as well as ΛCDM.
- Revised FIRAS calibration moving T_CMB outside 2.72548 ± 0.00057 K, or revised
  BBN/observational Y_p outside the ~0.247 range at high significance.
