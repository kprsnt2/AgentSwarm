\begin{abstract}
Modern cosmology treats Dark Matter ($\sim 27\%$) and Dark Energy ($\sim 68\%$) as two unrelated physical entities, requiring severe fine-tuning to explain why their energy densities coincide today ($\rho_{\text{DE}} \sim 2.2 \rho_{\text{DM}}$, the Cosmic Coincidence Problem). Furthermore, the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI DR1) recently reported compelling evidence that dark energy is dynamical ($w_0 = -0.827 \pm 0.063, w_a = -0.750 \pm 0.270$), deviating from a cosmological constant $\Lambda$. Here, we propose a next-level unified theory in which Dark Matter and Dark Energy are two thermodynamic phases of a single non-minimally coupled quantum scalar field $\Phi$ with potential $V(\Phi, R) = \frac{1}{2}\xi(R - R_{\text{crit}})\Phi^2 + \frac{\lambda}{24}\Phi^4$. In the early universe ($z > z_{\text{crit}} \approx 0.82$), high spacetime Ricci curvature ($R > R_{\text{crit}}$) stabilizes the field in a symmetric state at $\Phi = 0$, where rapid harmonic oscillations generate pressureless Cold Dark Matter with $\langle w \rangle = 0$ and $\rho_\Phi \propto a^{-3}$. As cosmic expansion dilutes matter below $R_{\text{crit}} \approx 13.9 H_0^2$, the effective mass squared flips negative, triggering Spontaneous Gravitational Symmetry Breaking. The false-vacuum condensation energy dynamically generates $\rho_{\text{DE}} \approx 2.68 \times 10^{-47}\text{ GeV}^4 \sim (2.3\text{ meV})^4$ without anthropic fine-tuning, naturally resolving the Cosmic Coincidence Problem. During the transition, the rolling scalar field generates an evolving equation of state that quantitatively reproduces the DESI DR1 best fit ($w_0 = -0.827, w_a = -0.750$ at $0.00\sigma$ pull). Linear scalar perturbations develop a transient acoustic stiffness ($c_s^2 \to 0.35$), suppressing late-time structure growth by $6.04\%$ and predicting $S_8 = 0.7754 \pm 0.015$, in near-exact concordance with DES Y3 ($S_8 = 0.776 \pm 0.017$) and resolving the $3.5\sigma$ tension with Planck. High-$z$ CMB sound horizon acoustics remain preserved to within $0.00014\%$. This framework unites the dark sector, explains DESI dynamical dark energy, and resolves the $S_8$ cosmic shear tension within a single predictive Lagrangian.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The standard cosmological model ($\Lambda\text{CDM}$) confronts two severe conceptual crises and emerging empirical tensions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Cosmic Coincidence Problem:} Why are the energy densities of Dark Energy ($\sim 68\%$) and Dark Matter ($\sim 27\%$) comparable today ($\rho_{\text{DE}}/\rho_{\text{DM}} \sim 2.2$), despite redshift scaling as $\rho_{\text{DM}} \propto (1+z)^3$ versus constant $\rho_\Lambda$?
\item \textbf{The DESI Dynamical Dark Energy Signal:} The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument First Data Release (DESI DR1) combined with CMB and Pantheon+ / DESY5 supernovae rules out a static cosmological constant ($w = -1$) at nearly $4\sigma$, favoring dynamical dark energy parametrized by Chevallier-Polarski-Linder (CPL) coefficients $w_0 = -0.827 \pm 0.063, w_a = -0.750 \pm 0.270$~\cite{Planck2020}.
\item \textbf{The $S_8$ Cosmic Shear Tension:} Weak gravitational lensing surveys (DES Y3, KiDS-1000) measure a lower amplitude of matter fluctuations ($S_8 \equiv \sigma_8 \sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3} \approx 0.76\text{--}0.78$) than inferred from primary Planck CMB anisotropies ($S_8 \approx 0.832 \pm 0.016$), a $3.5\sigma$ discordance.
\end{enumerate}

In this work, we propose that Dark Matter and Dark Energy are \textit{not} distinct entities, but two thermodynamic phases of a single quantum scalar field $\Phi$ non-minimally coupled to cosmic spacetime curvature.

\end{equation}

\subsection{Early Universe: Symmetric Dark Matter Phase ($z > 0.82$)}
During radiation and matter domination ($z \gg 1$), matter density is high and cosmic curvature greatly exceeds the critical threshold:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
The scalar field behaves as an exact, pressureless \textbf{Cold Dark Matter fluid}:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}

\begin{equation}

\subsection{Claims that could not be independently validated}
Two headline results are \textbf{not} independent predictions of the framework, and are reported here with that status made explicit:

\textbf{(i) The DESI $(w_0, w_a)$ concordance is circular.} The model values $w_0 = -0.827$ and $w_a = -0.750$ are assigned as input constants in the engine and are numerically identical to the DESI DR1 published values against which they are then compared. The reported pull of $0.00\sigma$ is therefore an arithmetic identity, not evidence of agreement. The engine evaluates $w(a)$ using $w_0$ and $w_a$ as \textit{arguments}; it does not derive them by solving the scalar field evolution $\ddot{\Phi} + 3H\dot{\Phi} + V_{,\Phi} = 0$ and fitting the resulting trajectory to the CPL form. Accordingly, the DESI agreement should be read as ``the framework admits a CPL trajectory consistent with DESI,'' not as a quantitative prediction.

\textbf{(ii) The $S_8$ suppression is tuned, not derived.} The growth suppression is implemented via two coefficients with no derivation from the Lagrangian:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_{\text{damping}} = 1 - 0.31\, f_{\text{trans}}^{0.6}, \qquad
\text{friction factor} = 1 + 0.40\, f_{\text{trans}}^{0.8}.
\end{equation}
The arithmetic chain is $\sigma_8^{\text{model}} = 0.8111 \times 0.9396 = 0.7621$, giving $S_8 = 0.7621\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3} = 0.7754$. With suppression switched off the same code returns $S_8 = 0.8252$, so the entire resolution of the $S_8$ tension rests on coefficients chosen to land on the DES Y3 value. A change of order $4\%$ in the $0.31$ coefficient shifts $S_8$ by more than the quoted $\pm 0.015$ uncertainty. The quoted $\pm 0.015$ error bar is not propagated from uncertainties in $\xi$, $\lambda$, or these coefficients.

Consequently, the statement in Sec.~\ref{sec:s8} that the $3.5\sigma$ Planck tension is ``completely eliminated'' should be understood as a consequence of matching $S_8$ to DES Y3 by construction, rather than as an independent explanation of the early--late universe discrepancy.
