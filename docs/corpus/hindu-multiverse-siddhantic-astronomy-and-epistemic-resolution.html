2.1. Lallācārya's Mathematical Refutation of Puranic Cosmography

In the Śiṣyadhīvṛddhida Tantra (c. 720–790 CE), Lallācārya devotes an entire chapter (Chapter 20: Mithyājñāna-nirākaraṇa / "Refutation of Erroneous Knowledge") to mathematically debunking Puranic cosmography:

  • **Primary Text (Śiṣyadhīvṛddhida Tantra 20.3):**
yadi vyomni tiṣṭhati tad-āśrayeṇa kṣitiḥ kiṁ na tiṣṭhati svata eva | "If the tortoise or serpent stands in empty space by divine support, why should the Earth not stand in empty space by its own inherent nature?"
  • **Refutation of Mount Meru (Śiṣyadhīvṛddhida 20.4–6):**

Lalla proves that if Mount Meru were 84,000 yojanas high above a flat Earth, it would cast an enormous shadow across the hemispheres, blocking the Sun and stars from human sight. Because the Sun is observed rising and setting across a spherical horizon (gola-bandha), the 84,000-yojana central mountain is physically impossible.

  • **Refutation of Concentric Liquid Oceans (Śiṣyadhīvṛddhida 20.32–34):**

Lalla points out that sailors and explorers traveling south and north encounter exclusively the salty ocean (lavaṇa-samudra), and that concentric oceans of milk, clarified butter, wine, and sugarcane juice are nowhere to be found in physical reality.

2.2. Āryabhaṭa and Brahmagupta's Empirical Demarcation

  • **Āryabhaṭa (Āryabhaṭīya, Golapāda 6–9):** Defines the Earth as a self-gravitating sphere (sarvato vṛttaḥ) suspended in space (antarikṣe), rotating on its axis once per sidereal day (bhramaṇam anena kriyate prātipadīkam).
  • **Brahmagupta (Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta 21.32):** Notes that just as water flows downward to the center of a spherical object, all heavy bodies fall toward the center of the Earth from all sides.
  • Astronomical Stance on the Multiverse: Because mathematical astronomy relied strictly on Pratyakṣa (direct empirical observation of celestial positions) and Anumāna (trigonometric deduction), astronomers like Āryabhaṭa, Lalla, and Brahmagupta remained completely agnostic or explicitly dismissive toward unobservable parallel universes (ananta-brahmāṇḍa), relegating them to theological poetic license (arthavāda).

3. Mathematical Derivation of the Brahmāṇḍa Boundary in Siddhāntic Astronomy

While the Puranas assert an arbitrary mythological diameter of 500,000,000 yojanas (50 crore yojanas) for the cosmic egg, mathematical astronomy derived an exact, computable cosmological boundary based on celestial kinematics.

3.1. The Principle of Uniform Mean Linear Velocity (Sama-gati)

In the Sūrya Siddhānta (Chapter 12, vv. 80–90) and Bhāskara II's Siddhānta Śiromaṇi (Golādhyāya, Bhuvanakośa), the fundamental postulate of planetary motion is that all planets travel through space at the exact same linear speed in yojanas per day: $$v_{\text{planet}} = \text{constant}$$

Because every planet moves at the same linear velocity, a planet with a larger orbit takes longer to complete one revolution. Hence, orbital circumferences are strictly inversely proportional to their revolution numbers in a Mahāyuga: $$C_{\text{planet}} = \frac{N_{\text{Moon}}}{N_{\text{planet}}} \times C_{\text{Moon}}$$

  • Concentric Sheaths (each 10× thicker):
  • Water (Ap): radial thickness $= 5 \times 10^9$ yojanas

  • Air (Vāyu): radial thickness $= 5 \times 10^{11}$ yojanas

  • Cosmic Ego (Ahaṅkāra): radial thickness $= 5 \times 10^{13}$ yojanas
  • Cosmic Intellect (Mahat): radial thickness $= 5 \times 10^{14}$ yojanas
  • Unmanifest Nature (Pradhāna): radial thickness $= 5 \times 10^{15}$ yojanas

5.1. Kumārila Bhaṭṭa’s Philosophical Demolition of Multiverse Cosmogony

In Ślokavārttika (Sambandhākṣepa-parihāra, vv. 40–115), Kumārila Bhaṭṭa mounts the most rigorous logical critique against multiverse cosmogenesis in Indian thought:

  1. **Regress of Demiurges (Anavasthā):** If each Brahmāṇḍa requires a conscious creator (Brahmā), who created that creator? Postulating an overarching creator (Mahā-Viṣṇu) creates an infinite regress.
  2. Absence of Motive for Creation: A supreme, complete being (āptakāma) cannot have an unfulfilled desire (kāma) to create trillions of universes filled with suffering.
  3. **Impossibility of Universal Dissolution (Mahāpralaya):** There is no empirical evidence (anupalabdhi) that the entire cosmos ever ceases to exist simultaneously. Individual living beings are born and die, but the cosmic background remains eternal and uncreated (anādi-ananta).

7. What is Established, What Remains Unknown, and Falsification Criteria

7.1. What Has Been Established With Definitive Certainty

  1. The Vocabulary of Parallel Universes Emerged Late:

The Vedic Samhitās (c. 1500–1000 BCE) do not contain the concept of parallel Brahmāṇḍas; they describe a single tripartite cosmos (dyauḥ-antarikṣa-pṛthivī). The spatial bubble multiverse (ananta-koṭi-brahmāṇḍa) emerged in the classical Epic-Puranic stratum (c. 300–900 CE).

  1. Indian Astronomy Rejected Puranic Flat-Earth and Parallel Eggs:

The mathematical astronomers (Jyotiṣa Siddhāntins) proved the Earth is a sphere, mathematically refuting Mount Meru and flat-earth lore. They defined a single empirical Brahmāṇḍa boundary (Kha-kakṣā = $1.8712 \times 10^{16}$ yojanas) and remained agnostic toward theological parallel universes.

  1. Quantitative Scale Convergence:

The outer boundary of the Puranic 7-sheath envelope ($5.556 \times 10^{15}$ yojanas / $7,558$ light-years) and the Siddhāntic Kha-kakṣā ($2.978 \times 10^{15}$ yojanas / $4,051$ light-years) converge within a factor of $1.87\times$, enabling commentarial harmonization between empirical astronomy and sacred lore.

  1. Orthodox Rejection Existed:

Orthodox Hindu thought was not universally multiverse-affirming. Pūrva Mīmāṁsā (Kumārila Bhaṭṭa) systematically rejected cosmic creation, cosmic dissolution, and parallel universes, defending an uncreated steady-state eternal cosmos.

  1. Epistemic Non-Equivalence with Modern Physics:

Sanskrit multiverse descriptions are theological, mythological, and phenomenological architectures. While philosophically profound and structurally astonishing, they do not constitute mathematical physics, and conflating them violates basic epistemic standards.

7.2. What Remains Unknown / Open for Research

  1. The Exact Transmission Path of the 50-Crore Yojana Figure:

The precise historical transition that shifted the astronomical calculation of planetary orbits into the fixed 500,000,000 yojana Puranic figure remains textually debated among Indologists (Pingree vs. Plofker).

  1. Cross-Pollination Between Tantric Bhuvana Geometry and Siddhāntic Spherical Astronomy:

The degree to which late medieval Kashmiri and Keralite astronomers (e.g., Mādhava of Saṅgamagrāma, Nīlakaṇṭha Somayājī) cross-referenced Tantric Bhuvana texts during their mathematical revisions of planetary anomalies.
