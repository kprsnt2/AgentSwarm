2.2 The Ontological Paradox of Pārthiva Aṇḍa: 108 Worlds in a Single Tattva

A startling philological discovery in Tantric cosmology is that the entire Purāṇic cosmos—the 14 planetary spheres (Caturdaśa-bhuvana), Mount Meru, the seven subterranean realms (Atala, Vitala, Sutala...), and the seven celestial heavens (Bhūr, Bhuvar, Svar, Mahar, Jana, Tapas, Satya)—is confined strictly to the lowest, densest single Tattva: Pṛthvī (gross matter)!

$$\text{All Purāṇic Brahmāṇḍas} \subset \text{Pārthiva Aṇḍa} = \text{Tattva 36 (Pṛthvī alone!)}$$

Within this single Tattva, Abhinavagupta catalogs 108 Bhuvanas:

  • Bhuvanas 1–8: The subterranean foundations governed by Kālāgnirudra.
  • Bhuvanas 9–56: The subterranean hell realms (Naraka) and underworlds (Pātāla).
  • Bhuvanas 57–70: The terrestrial regions of Jambūdvīpa, the ocean concentric rings, and mountain ranges.
  • Bhuvanas 71–108: The celestial atmospheric and heavenly planetary systems up to the outer shell of Brahmā's egg.

Beyond this gross sphere lie the 116 higher worlds (56 Prākṛta + 28 Māyīya + 32 Śākta), meaning that in Tantric cosmology, physical material existence comprises less than half ($48.2\%$) of the total cataloged cosmic domains!

4.1 The Mīmāṃsā Refutation of Cosmic Multiverses (Kumārila Bhaṭṭa, c. 650–700 CE)

The orthodox school of Vedic ritual hermeneutics, Pūrva Mīmāṃsā, is staunchly anti-cosmological and anti-creationist. In his Ślokavārttika (Sambandhākṣepaparihāra, verses 113–117), Kumārila Bhaṭṭa systematically dismantles both the creator God and the cyclic creation/dissolution of worlds:

$$\text{न कदाचिदनीदृशं जगत् (Na kadācid anīdṛśaṁ jagat)}$$

"At no time was the world unlike it is now." (Ślokavārttika 113)

Kumārila's Three Epistemic Arguments Against Multiverses & Dissolution:

  1. **The Argument from Missing Eyewitness (Pratyakṣa-anupalabdhi):** Nobody was present before creation to witness God creating the universe or destroying all worlds. Any scripture describing creation is either secondary narrative hyperbole (arthavāda) or unprovable assertion.
  2. **The Argument from the Eternity of the Veda (Śabda-nityatva):** If the universe were completely destroyed in a Mahāpralaya, the lineage of teachers reciting the eternal Sanskrit words (śabda) would be wiped out. Since the Veda is uncreated (apauruṣeya) and eternal, there can never have been a time when society and teachers did not exist.
  3. The Argument Against Cosmic Demiurges: If a God created the universe, why did He create pain and suffering? If because of past karma, then previous beings already existed, meaning creation had no beginning!

Epistemic Verdict: Within orthodox Brahmanical scholasticism, the Mīmāṃsā school completely rejected the Purāṇic multiverse, declaring the universe to be a perpetual, singular, uncreated steady-state system.

4.2 The Advaita vs. Dvaita Multiverse Schism

Within Vedānta, the nature of the multiverse forms a central battleground:

  • Advaita Vedānta (Śaṅkarācārya, c. 788–820 CE):
  • Universes are Māyā-kārya (products of nescience).
  • Multiple universes (aneka-brahmāṇḍa) are accepted at the Vyāvahārika (empirical/practical) level to explain the diverse karmic fruits of different jīvas.
  • However, at the Pāramārthika (ultimate) level, no universe has ever been born (Ajātivāda of Gauḍapāda). The multiverse is an apparent superimposition (vivarta) on Brahman.
  • Dvaita Vedānta (Madhvācārya, c. 1238–1317 CE):
  • Rejects vivarta and māyā. Matter (Prakṛti) and souls are distinct, eternal realities (Pañcabheda).
  • Madhvā asserts the objective, eternal reality of infinite Brahmāṇḍas (ananta-brahmāṇḍa-satya-vāda).
  • Each Brahmāṇḍa is physically real, distinct from all others, and governed by its own Brahmā, all subordinate to the supreme independent will of Lord Nārāyaṇa.

  4. The Mīmāṃsā Dissent: Hindu thought was not monolithically multiverse-affirming; Pūrva Mīmāṃsā established a rigorous, steady-state, eternal-cosmos model rejecting creation, dissolution, and parallel universes.
  5. String Theory Conflation is a Category Error: The 36 Tattvas are phenomenological states of awareness, not Riemannian spatial dimensions of Planck-scale physics.
