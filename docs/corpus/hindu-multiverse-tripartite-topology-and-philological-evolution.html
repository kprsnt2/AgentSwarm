4. Architecture III: The Cyclic Temporal Multiverse & The Kalpa-Bheda Principle

4.1 Primary Textual Documentation: The Kalpa-Bheda Doctrine

In Western philosophy, cyclic cosmologies (such as the Stoic ekpyrōsis or Nietzsche's Eternal Recurrence) are often assumed to be strictly deterministic and identically repeating.

Classical Hindu literature, however, recognized that if every cosmic cycle were identical, variations in sacred narratives across different texts would represent factual errors or contradictions. To resolve this, the Purāṇas formulated the Doctrine of Kalpa-Bheda (Variance across Cosmic Cycles).

In Matsya Purāṇa 290.3–6: $$\text{कल्पभेदेन वृत्तान्ता भिन्नाः स्युः सर्ववस्तुषु ।}$$ $$\text{पुराणेषु क्वचित्क्वचिद् दृश्यन्ते विविधाः कथाः ॥ ३ ॥}$$ kalpa-bhedena vṛttāntā bhinnāḥ syuḥ sarva-vastuṣu / purāṇeṣu kvacit kvacid dṛśyante vividhāḥ kathāḥ

Translation: "Due to variations across different Kalpas (kalpa-bhedena), historical events and circumstances differ in all respects. Therefore, in the various Purāṇas, divergent narratives are found in various places."

In Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.22.81: $$\text{यथर्तुलिङ्गान्यृतवः सर्व एव भवन्ति वै ।}$$ $$\text{तथैव भावा जायन्ते ते ते तस्मिंस्तथा तथा ॥}$$ "Just as the distinctive characteristics of each season reappear naturally when the season returns, so do the beings and situations reappear in each cycle, yet according to their own conditioned development."
