2.1 The Critical Heliodorus Textual Link

This is an exact verbatim citation of Mahabharata 5.43.22: $$\text{"दमस्त्यागोऽप्रमादश्च एतेष्वमृतमाहितम् ।"}$$ Epistemic Adjudication: This proves that by 113 BCE, not only was Krishna-Vasudeva worshipped as Devadeva (God of Gods) by foreign Greek dignitaries, but the didactic philosophical chapters of the Mahabharata were already circulating in standardized, memorable poetic forms across North-Central India.

4. Frontier 3: Critical Epistemic Adjudication of Archaeoastronomy

Popular revisionist accounts (e.g. P.V. Vartak, Nilesh Oak) claim astronomical dating can prove the war occurred in 5561 BCE or 3067 BCE. We evaluate these claims against rigorous celestial mechanics, textual criticism, and material archaeology.

4.1 Falsification of the 5561 BCE Hypothesis (Nilesh Oak / P.V. Vartak)

The claim that the Mahabharata War occurred in 5561 BCE rests almost entirely on a single half-verse from Bhishma Parva 2.31: $$\text{"अरुन्धती तयाप्येष वसिष्ठः पृष्ठतः कृतः"}$$ (“Even Arundhati has placed Vasistha behind her.”)

Oak argues that due to stellar proper motion and axial precession, Alcor (Arundhati) had a lower Right Ascension than Mizar (Vasistha) between 11,091 BCE and 4508 BCE, and concludes the war occurred in 5561 BCE.

The Epistemic & Methodological Refutation:

  1. Hermeneutic Violation (The Omen Context): Bhishma Parva chapters 2 and 3 do not record observational astronomical tables; they record a desperate catalog of **apocalyptic bad omens (Utpatas)** presented by Vyasa to Dhritarashtra to warn of catastrophe. In the identical speech, Vyasa records:
  2. Cows giving birth to donkeys and jackals (Bhishma 3.3).
  3. Holy temple statues laughing, crying blood, and falling (Bhishma 2.26).
  4. Mares giving birth to cows (Bhishma 3.4).
  5. Rahu seizing the sun and moon out of season on the 13th day (Bhishma 3.11).

Isolating the Arundhati line as an infallible scientific measurement while discarding the talking statues and donkey-calves is textbook cherry-picking bias.

  1. Planetary Contradictions: In the exact same passage (Bhishma 2.32), Saturn is stated to be afflicting Rohini, while elsewhere Mars is retrograde in Magha and Jupiter in Shravana. In 5561 BCE, these planetary positions are astronomically impossible. Proponents simply dismiss dozens of contradictory planetary positions as "metaphors" or "interpolations" while treating their favored verse as absolute.
  2. Archaeological Absurdity: In 5561 BCE, the Indian subcontinent was in the aceramic Neolithic phase (Mehrgarh Period I). Smelted bloomery iron (naraca) did not appear until 4,200 years later; spoked war chariots were not invented until 3,500 years later; Indo-Aryan Sanskrit syntax did not appear until 4,000 years later. Asserting a 5561 BCE date requires postulating an advanced Iron Age chariot civilization that vanished without leaving a single microscopic scrap of bronze, iron, bone, pottery, or seed anywhere in Eurasia.
