Detailed Claim Analysis:

  1. $C_1$ (Metaphysical Claim): Rooted in the Bhagavad Gītā (4.7–8: "yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata..."; 10.8: "ahaṁ sarvasya prabhavo mattaḥ sarvaṁ pravartate").   2. $C_2$ (Historical-Biographical Claim): Rooted in early non-epic and sub-epic strata, notably Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.17.6 (referencing Kṛṣṇa Devakīputra as a student of sage Ghora Āṅgirasa) and Pāṇini’s Aṣṭādhyāyī 4.3.98 (referencing Vāsudevaka, devotees of Vāsudeva). This claim asserts that behind the later deified figure stands an actual Late Bronze/Early Iron Age human protagonist.
  3. $C_3$ (Historical-Event Claim): Rooted in the Jaya core (8,800 verses attributed to Vyāsa in Mahābhārata 1.1.81). This asserts that the Kuru kingdom of the Late Vedic period suffered a destructive civil war between cousin factions over control of Hastinapur and Kurukṣetra.
  4. $C_4$ (Epic-Demographic Claim): Rooted in Mahābhārata 5.155 (the mobilization of 11 Kaurava and 7 Pāṇḍava Akṣauhiṇīs). This asserts a logistical and military mobilization dwarfing World War II armies concentrated into an area smaller than modern metropolitan Delhi.

$$\text{Recovery Fraction} = (1 - 0.95) \times 0.02 = 0.001 \quad (0.1\%)$$ Nine hundred and ninety-nine out of every thousand casualties were physically dissolved into carbon dioxide and river silt within decades of the battle.

Section 4: What Has Been Established, What Remains Unknown, and What Would Change Our Mind

1. What Has Been Established:

  • Material-Geographical Grounding: All major geographical sites mentioned in the core epic (Hastinapur, Kurukṣetra, Tilpat, Mathura, Ahichchhatra, Kampilya) possess real, stratified Painted Grey Ware (PGW) archaeological horizons dating to c. 1100–600 BCE. The epic is not set in a mythical Neverland, but in an authentic Iron Age landscape.
  • Geomorphological Concordance: The flood stratum at Hastinapur identified by B.B. Lal directly matches the textual account in the Purāṇas of the capital being abandoned by King Nicakṣu due to a Gaṅgā flood, moving the seat to Kauśāmbī.
  • Marine Architecture at Dvārakā: Marine archaeology at Bet Dwarka has uncovered submerged Late Bronze / Early Iron Age fortifications, stone anchors (3-holed Mediterranean/Levantine type), and Lustrous Red Ware (c. 1500–1000 BCE), confirming a maritime trading port that suffered coastal submergence.
  • Epigraphic Horizon of Cultic Radiance: Epigraphic and numismatic records confirm that by the 2nd century BCE, Krishna-Vāsudeva was actively worshipped across a 1,400 km radius: from the Heliodoros pillar at Besnagar (113 BCE) to Agathocles drachms at Ai-Khanoum, Bactria (185 BCE), Chilas petroglyphs (50 BCE), and the Mora Well inscription at Mathura (15 CE).
  • Falsification of Atomic Claims: Radiation dosimetry and mass spectrometry of soil samples at Kurukṣetra confirm standard crustal background radioactivity ($0.10–0.14\ \mu\text{Sv/h}$) and zero anthropogenic radioisotopes ($^{137}\text{Cs}$, $^{90}\text{Sr}$, $^{239}\text{Pu}$), disproving claims of ancient nuclear detonations.

2. What Remains Unknown (and Structurally Indeterminate):

  • Individual Biological Identity: Whether an individual human skeleton can ever be definitively labeled as "Krishna" or "Arjuna" remains impossible without contemporary royal cartouches or unbroken inscriptional lineages, neither of which exists for 1000 BCE India.
  • Exact Operational Casualty Numbers: Whether the original battle involved 5,000 men or 50,000 men cannot be recovered; the original numbers were permanently magnified by the transition from Jaya to the encyclopedic Mahābhārata.
  • Theological Reality of the Avatāra: Whether Krishna was a manifestation of God is a matter of religious faith, theology, and spiritual experience, intrinsically beyond the jurisdiction of empirical archaeology, physics, or carbon dating.
