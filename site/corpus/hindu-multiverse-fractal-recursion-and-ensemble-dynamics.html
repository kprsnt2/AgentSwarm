1. Executive Summary & Epistemic Scope

Across the corpus of classical Indian thought, the question of "multiple universes" is neither homogenous nor monolithic. While modern popular discourse and concordist apologetics treat the "Hindu multiverse" as an undifferentiated confirmation of contemporary quantum or inflationary physics, rigorous philological and historical analysis reveals a far richer, multifaceted dialectic.

This investigation delivers three decisive breakthroughs in the textual and historical analysis of Hindu cosmology:

  1. The Fourfold Multiverse Typology:

We formally classify and demarcate the four radically distinct models of cosmic plurality documented across Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain textual corpora:

  • **Type II: Temporal-Cyclic Multiverse (Kramika Anādi-Ananta Kalpa-Cakra):** Sequentially oscillating universes across beginningless and endless time (Brahma Sūtras, Viṣṇu Purāṇa).
  • **Type III: Fractal-Recursive Multiverse (Paramāṇv-antar-gata Cid-Ākāśa Jagat):** Infinitely recursive nested universes existing within every subatomic particle and within every conscious mind (Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha, Brahma-saṃhitā).
  • **Type IV: Hierarchical-Dimensional Multiverse (Tattva-Adhva Bhuvana-Mālā):** Vertical ontic planes where billions of physical universes are subordinated to higher metaphysical vibrations (Tantrāloka, Svacchandatantra).

2. Formalization of the Fourfold Multiverse Typology

In classical Sanskrit literature, distinct philosophical traditions developed completely different architectures of plural worlds. Conflating them into a single generic "multiverse" obscures their unique epistemic and ontological functions.

2.1 Type I: Spatial-Parallel Multiverse (Saha-bhāvī Ananta-Koṭi-Brahmāṇḍa)

  • Primary Sanskrit Attestations:
  • Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.14.11:

$$\text{क्व चाहं तम-उद्भवोऽहं-महद्-ख-वाताग्नि-वार-भू-वेष्टित-अण्ड-घट-सप्त-वितस्ति-कायः ।}$$ $$\text{क्व चेदृग्-विधाविगणित-अण्ड-पराणु-चर्या-वाताध्व-रोम-विवरस्य च ते महित्वम् ॥}$$ (Where am I—a small four-headed Brahmā enclosed in an egg made of Mahat, Ahaṅkāra, ether, air, fire, water, and earth, measuring only seven spans—and where is Your limitless majesty, through whose pore-openings innumerable universes swirl like dust particles blowing through a window mesh?)

  • Brahma-vaivarta Purāṇa, Brahma-khaṇḍa 4.4.78–83:

$$\text{यथा काचा-घटानां च राशयो दृश्यन्ते जले । तथैव विश्व-सङ्घाश्च तिष्ठन्ति प्रकृति-जले ॥}$$ (Just as heaps of glass jars are seen submerged or floating in water, so collections of entire universes rest in the waters of primordial Prakṛti.)

  • Ontological Nature: Spatially concurrent, physical/subtle bubble-cosmoi (Brahmāṇḍa-koṭi), each enveloped in seven impenetrable material sheaths (sapta-āvaraṇa), floating in the Causal Ocean (Kāraṇodaka).
  • Causal Boundary: Completely isolated. Matter, light, and mortal beings cannot pass between envelopes; each possesses its own local space, time, and Demiurge (Brahmā).

2.2 Type II: Temporal-Cyclic Multiverse (Kramika Anādi-Ananta Kalpa-Cakra)

  • Primary Sanskrit Attestations:
  • Brahma Sūtras 2.1.35:

$$\text{न कर्माविभागादिति चेन्नानादित्वात् ॥ ३५ ॥}$$ (If it be argued that karma could not exist at creation due to non-differentiation, we refute it: because creation is beginningless.)

  • Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.2.65:

$$\text{यथा ससर्ज प्रक् सृष्टिं नृप कल्पादिसु प्रभुः । तथैव संहरत्यन्ते पुनः पुनः सनातनः ॥}$$ (Just as the Sovereign Lord emitted creation at the beginning of previous Kalpas, so He withdraws and regenerates it at the end, again and again, eternally.)

  • Ontological Nature: A single spacetime manifold undergoing continuous, oscillatory expansion (Sṛṣṭi), maintenance (Sthiti), and universal collapse (Pralaya / Mahāpralaya).
  • Causal Boundary: Strictly temporal. The universes are separated by temporal barriers of dissolution (Laya), but connected causally by the unbroken continuum of latent karmic impressions (saṃskāras / adṛṣṭa).

2.3 Type III: Fractal-Recursive Multiverse (Paramāṇv-antar-gata Cid-Ākāśa Jagat)

  • Primary Sanskrit Attestations:
  • Brahma-saṃhitā 5.35:

$$\text{एकोऽप्यसौ रचयितुं जगदण्डकोटिं यच्छक्तिरस्ति जगदण्डचया यदन्तः ।}$$ $$\text{अण्डान्तरस्थपरमाणुचयान्तरस्थं गोविन्दमादिपुरुषं तमहं भजामि ॥ ३५ ॥}$$ (He who, by His singular potency, creates millions of cosmic eggs, within which exist further collections of universes, and who enters each universe and resides within every atom [paramāṇu]—that Primeval Lord Govinda I worship.)

  • Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha, Nirvāṇa-prakaraṇa (Pūrvārdha) 6.2.36–38:

$$\text{प्रतिपरमाणुगर्भेषु सन्ति लोकाः परस्परम् । अनन्ययापि संसक्त-व्योमवद् भ्रान्तिमात्रतः ॥}$$ $$\text{तत्र सूर्य-शशाङ्काद्या देवा भूधर-सागराः । सर्वं वर्तत एवेदमविचित्रं महाधियाम् ॥}$$ (Inside the womb of every single atom, there exist complete worlds. Although interpenetrating like empty space, they remain mutually unaware of one another due to perceptual illusion. Within each atom, there are suns, moons, gods, mountains, and oceans, all existing seamlessly.)

  • Ontological Nature: Recursive, holographic nesting of complete cosmic realities within physical atoms and consciousness-points (Cid-aṇu).
  • Causal Boundary: Impermeable to gross mechanical instruments, but completely transparent to non-dual conscious apprehension (Jñāna / Samādhi).

2.4 Type IV: Hierarchical-Dimensional Multiverse (Tattva-Adhva Bhuvana-Mālā)

  • Primary Sanskrit Attestations:
  • Tantrāloka of Abhinavagupta (Ch. 8):

$$\text{पार्थिवेऽण्डान्तरे येयं ब्रह्माण्डानां परम्परा । तदूर्ध्वं तु ततोऽप्यन्या जलतत्त्वादि-संस्थिता ॥}$$ (This entire sequence of Brahmāṇḍas that we perceive is contained entirely within the physical/earthly egg [Pārthiva-Aṇḍa]. Above it, in the water-tattva and higher strata, exist vastly superior and greater series of worlds.)

  • Svacchandatantra (Paṭala 10.1120–1128):

$$\text{षट्त्रिंशत्तत्त्वसंयुक्ता भुवनानां शतत्रयम् । अष्टादशाधिकं चैव कलाध्वनि व्यवस्थितम् ॥}$$ (Distributed across the 36 Tattvas are 318 distinct cosmic realms [Bhuvanas], arranged systematically along the pathway of cosmic energy [Kalādhvan].)

  • Ontological Nature: A multi-dimensional vertical pyramid where lower realms are sub-sets of higher realms. The entire Puranic multiverse of 14 Lokas and billions of Brahmāṇḍas constitutes merely the 1st (lowest) Tattva (Pṛthvī-tattva).
  • Causal Boundary: Graded by the frequency of consciousness (Spanda). A soul cannot ascend to higher Bhuvanas without shedding lower material impurities (Malas).

3.3 Epistemic Consequence: Supra-Cosmic Non-Ergodicity

In modern statistical physics, an ergodic system visits all microstates over time, and time averages equal ensemble averages. In Puranic cosmography:

  • An individual universe is strictly non-stationary, dissipative, and finite; its entropy increases until total collapse at $t = \tau_{\text{lifespan}}$.

4. Fractal Cosmography: Worlds Within the Atom (Paramāṇu-Garbha)

The most radical assertion of cosmic plurality in Hindu texts appears in the Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha and Brahma-saṃhitā: **entire, complete universes exist inside every single subatomic particle (paramāṇu)**.

4.1 The Līlopākhyāna Formulation (Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha 3.14–3.30)

In the famous narrative of Queen Līlā and Goddess Sarasvatī:

  • King Padma dies. Līlā laments. Sarasvatī reveals that Padma’s soul has not traveled anywhere physically; his soul is seated in the empty space of the room's ether (gṛhākāśa), experiencing a brand new empire with mountains, subjects, and rivers.
  • Sarasvatī guides Līlā into the sub-space of the heart (cillīna-ākāśa) and the sub-space of an atom (paramāṇu-vivara).
  • Līlā discovers that within an area smaller than a mustard seed, an infinite cosmos exists, complete with its own Brahmā, its own solar systems, and millions of living beings who believe their universe is millions of yojanas wide!

5.1 Pūrva Mīmāṃsā: Kumārila Bhaṭṭa’s Fierce Anti-Multiverse Stance

In the Ślokavārttika (section Sambandhākṣepaparihāra, verses 45–114), Kumārila Bhaṭṭa mounts a devastating critique of all creation narratives, Demiurges, and plural universes:

$$\text{न कदाचिदनीदृशं जगत् ॥ ११३ ॥}$$ (The universe was never other than it is right now: it was never created, and it will never be destroyed.)

  • The Mīmāṃsaka Argument:
  • If God or Mahā-Viṣṇu created the universe, what was His motive? If He is perfect, He has no desire (prayojana). If He has desire, He is imperfect.
  • If He created the universe out of compassion (kṛpā), why did He create pain, suffering, tigers, and disease?
  • If souls suffered due to their past karma, then karma existed before creation! But if karma existed before creation, there was no "first creation," meaning the world is beginningless and uncreated!
  • Therefore, the concept of a cosmic creator blowing bubble universes from His skin pores is an irrational myth (arthavāda). The physical world is an uncreated, eternal steady-state.

5.2 Buddhist Abhidharma: Vasubandhu’s Decentralized Collective Karma

In the Abhidharmakośa (Chapter 3, Lokanirdeśa), Vasubandhu formulates a massive, decentralized cosmic plurality:

  • Tri-sāhasra-mahā-sāhasra-lokadhātu (Great Trichiliocosm):
  • A single world system ($10^0 = 1$ world) consists of Mount Meru, four continents, sun, moon, and six desire heavens.
  • A Sāhasra-cūḍika-lokadhātu contains $1,000$ world systems ($10^3$).
  • A Dvi-sāhasra-madhyama-lokadhātu contains $1,000 \times 1,000 = 1,000,000$ world systems ($10^6$).
  • A Tri-sāhasra-mahā-sāhasra-lokadhātu contains $1,000 \times 1,000,000 = 1,000,000,000$ world systems ($10^9$).
  • Radical Decentralization: There is NO God, NO Mahā-Viṣṇu, and NO primordial creator. The entire billion-world cluster is precipitated purely by the collective momentum of the karma of all sentient beings (sarva-sattvānāṁ karma-vaśena). When the karma ripens, wind-disks stir in space, condensing into matter.

5.3 Jainism: Umāsvāti’s Static, Concentric Ring Universe

In the Tattvārtha Sūtra (Chapters 3 and 4), Umāsvāti presents a radically different cosmological model:

  • The cosmos is not a collection of isolated eggs or bubbles. There is only one eternal, uncreated structure called Loka-Ākāśa, shaped like a standing human figure (narrow at the waist, broad at the top and bottom).
  • Plurality is achieved not by separate bubble universes, but by **concentric, alternating rings of continents (dvīpas) and oceans (samudras)** in the Middle World (Madhya-loka):
  • Jambūdvīpa ($100,000\text{ yojanas}$) surrounded by Lavaṇasamudra ($200,000\text{ yojanas}$), followed by Dhātakīkhaṇḍa ($400,000\text{ yojanas}$), continuing outward in exponential doubling across countless rings (asaṅkhyāta-dvīpa).
