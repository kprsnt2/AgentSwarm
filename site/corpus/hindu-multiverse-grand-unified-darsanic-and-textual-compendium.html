7.1. What Has Been Established With Definitive Certainty

  1. The Vedic Samhitās Did Not Teach a Multiverse:

The earliest stratum (c. 1500–1000 BCE) conceives strictly of a single tripartite universe (Triloka). Parallel Brahmāṇḍas do not appear until the Epic-Puranic era (c. 400 BCE – 500 CE).

  1. Internal Indian Philosophical Rejection Existed:

Hinduism never maintained a monolithic consensus on cosmic plurality. Orthodox Pūrva Mīmāṁsā and materialist Cārvāka strictly denied parallel universes and cosmic creation, while Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika restricted reality to a single cyclical egg.

  1. Five Distinct Typologies Co-Existed:

Sanskrit literature houses five distinct structural models: Cyclic-Temporal (Purāṇas), Spatial-Bubble (Bhāgavata/Vaiṣṇava), Phenomenological-Idealist (Yoga-Vāsiṣṭha), Hierarchical-Dimensional (Tantras), and Fractal-Recursive (Brahma-saṁhitā).

  1. Astronomy Diverged From Theology:

Mathematical astronomers (Jyotiṣa Siddhāntins) demonstrated the Earth is a sphere, mathematically refuted Mount Meru, and restricted calculations to a single empirical cosmos (Kha-kakṣā).

Equating ancient mythological or phenomenological frameworks with modern quantum mechanics, general relativity, or inflationary cosmology violates standard epistemic criteria and historical philology.
