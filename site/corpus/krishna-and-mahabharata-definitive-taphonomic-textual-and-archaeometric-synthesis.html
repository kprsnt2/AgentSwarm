The question of whether Lord Krishna was a real historical individual and whether the Mahābhārata war occurred cannot be answered with a monolithic "yes" or "no" without committing fundamental epistemic category errors. A rigorous historical-critical inquiry requires parsing the composite epic into its constituent historical, epigraphic, archaeological, and theological strata.

He served as an allied diplomat and advisor in an intra-tribal succession conflict between Kuru lineages (the historical core of the Mahābhārata war) in the Kurukṣetra region of the upper Gaṅgā-Yamunā doab.
  2. Textual and Mythological Stratigraphy: The epic expanded over a 1,300-year redaction window from an archaic heroic ballad (Jaya, ~8,800 verses) to an expanded dynastic chronicle (Bhārata, ~24,000 verses), culminating in an encyclopedic dharma and cosmic compendium (Mahābhārata, ~100,000 verses) completed during the Gupta period (c. 4th century CE).
  3. Epigraphic Deification Sequence: Inscriptional and numismatic records directly demonstrate the sequential apotheosis of Krishna: from a human Vedic seeker (Chāndogya Upaniṣad, c. 650 BCE) to a venerated hero (Pāṇini, c. 450 BCE), to an iconographic divinity on coins (Agathocles, c. 185 BCE), to the supreme God of Gods (Heliodoros column, 113 BCE), to one of the five deified human Vṛṣṇi heroes (Mora Well inscription, c. 15 CE).

Section 3: Textual Stratigraphy and the Epigraphic Deification Trajectory

The Mahābhārata Critical Edition (BORI, edited by V.S. Sukthankar et al., 1933–1959) analyzed 1,259 manuscripts across multiple scripts, establishing that the epic is an accretive, multi-layered text.

The Three Structural Epic Strata:

  1. Jaya (Victory Ballad): $\approx 8,800$ verses (attested in MBh 1.1.81: aṣṭau śloka-sahasrāṇi aṣṭau śloka-śatāni ca). Focuses on the military tactical conflict between the Kauravas and Pāṇḍavas. Krishna appears primarily as a mortal ally, diplomat, and counselor.
  2. Bhārata: $\approx 24,000$ verses (MBh 1.1.61: caturviṁśati-sāhasrīṁ cakre bhārata-saṁhitām). Expands the dynastic genealogy, heroic codes, and ethical debates. Krishna is elevated to a legendary hero and divine-human teacher.
  3. Mahābhārata (Śatasahasrī Saṁhitā): $\approx 100,000$ verses (attested in Gupta-era inscriptions c. 450 CE). Incorporates vast encyclopedic treatises (Śāntiparvan, Anuśāsanaparvan) and the Harivaṁśa appendix. Krishna is fully established as the supreme cosmic Svayam Bhagavān and universal soul (Viśvarūpa).
