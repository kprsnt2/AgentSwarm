The Transformation: Pañcavīra $\to$ Caturvyūha $\to$ Triad

  1. **The Original Five Vṛṣṇi Heroes (Pañcavīrāḥ):**

The Mora Well Inscription (reign of Mahākṣatrapa Śoḍāsa, c. 15 CE) specifically attests the worship of five mortal heroes of the Vṛṣṇi lineage:

  • Saṅkarṣaṇa / Balarāma (Elder brother, son of Rohiṇī)
  • Vāsudeva / Kṛṣṇa (Leader, son of Devakī)
  • Pradyumna (Son of Vāsudeva and Rukmiṇī)
  • Śāmba (Son of Vāsudeva and Jāmbavatī)
  • Aniruddha (Grandson of Vāsudeva, son of Pradyumna)
  • The Systematic Exclusion of Śāmba:

When the Pāñcarātra theological school developed the doctrine of cosmic emanations (Caturvyūha), Śāmba was explicitly purged:

  • Genealogical Barrier: Śāmba's mother Jāmbavatī was an outsider (daughter of the non-Aryan/Ṛkṣa tribal leader Jāmbavān).
  • Mythological Blame: In the Mausala Parva (16.1–3), Śāmba is blamed for the doom of the Yādavas by insulting the sages with an iron mace strapped to his abdomen.
  • Foreign Religious Associations: Śāmba was associated with Iranian solar cults (the Maga Brāhmaṇas brought from Śākadvīpa to cure his leprosy, as recounted in the Śāmba Purāṇa and Bhaviṣya Purāṇa).
  • Philosophical Schematization: The theologians required four emanations to map onto the four psychological/cosmic faculties: Vāsudeva (Supreme Self), Saṅkarṣaṇa (Individual Soul / Jīva), Pradyumna (Mind / Manas), and Aniruddha (Ego / Ahaṅkāra).
  • The Rise of the Triad (Jagannātha, Balabhadra, Subhadrā):

Simultaneously, folk tradition paired the two leading brothers (Saṅkarṣaṇa and Vāsudeva) with their sister Ekānaṁśā (later Subhadrā), as seen in Kuṣāṇa-era reliefs at Devangarh and Mathura, directly laying the iconographic foundation for the Jagannātha triad at Puri.
